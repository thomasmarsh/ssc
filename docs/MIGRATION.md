# C++ to Rust reimplementation

The original C++ prototype lives in git history; the implementation lives in `src/`. The Rust project separates gameplay simulation from its Bevy desktop adapter so that core rules can be iterated on and exercised without opening a window. Bevy 0.19.1 supplies the desktop app, input, UI, and WGPU rendering; native macOS rendering uses Metal. Shapes and interface elements are generated in code, so the new app does not depend on the prototype's OpenGL asset pipeline.

## Gameplay carried forward

The prototype is a top-down arena shooter. The player steers a ship, fires at enemy waves, and advances after clearing the Bogeys. Its default arena is a fixed 4000 by 4000 screen with solid edges. The Rust version uses a simpler 2D model, keeps the ship, flocking enemies, firing and collisions, and replaces the fixed arena with a large panning universe.

## Reimplementation choices

- Gameplay rules live in a plain Rust simulation module; Bevy systems handle frame timing, input, rendering, and UI.
- Movement and contact handling use a small 2D model rather than porting the prototype's ODE rigid-body setup.
- The renderer uses procedural 2D geometry and Bevy UI instead of the prototype's OpenGL meshes, textures, and camera system.
- The world is unbounded and the camera follows the ship. It is divided into 6000-unit sectors, with sector (0, 0) centered on the origin; the HUD shows the current one and the radar shows the neighborhood. The simulation runs at 60 Hz. Pause stops ticks; slow motion scales their duration.
- A sector's contents are a pure function of the world seed and its coordinates. The coordinates map to a continuous latent parameter vector (danger, aggression, density, distortion, tech, swarm), and a generation policy turns that vector, plus the sector's gene pool, into asteroids, gravity wells and creature populations. Creatures are species with genomes, not enumerated kinds. Species are placed by niches (`src/range.rs`: depth profile x patch mask x biome affinity, capped to the strongest few per sector; `src/biome.rs`), not per sector. Sector (0, 0) is HOME, a peaceful start with no creatures, pinned by a golden test; Fatsos fill ring 1, Bogeys join them on ring 2 and Lunatics debut on ring 3. See [UNIVERSE.md](UNIVERSE.md).
- Only sectors overlapping an active region around the player (`ACTIVE_HALF`, larger than the screen) are loaded and simulated, so loading happens off screen. Bodies that wander into unloaded sectors freeze, and sectors more than two away are dropped and regenerated on return (kills persist by stable spawn index). Enemies are not bound to their sector: an alerted enemy keeps chasing the player across borders.
- Enemies live rather than wait, and flocking is emergent: there are no flock objects or memberships. Each creature reacts only to same-species neighbors within 380 units (heading alignment, loose cohesion that only pulls stragglers back, and a wide personal space), so nearby bands merge into one drifting crowd and split apart again through individual restlessness and pace. Passive schoolers (a school that waits to be provoked, the Bogey) hold together harder: stragglers are pulled back from 170 units instead of 220, pace varies less and they fidget less, so a band stays a band (see `start_bogeys_stay_in_schools_and_stay_calm`, which watches the ring-two school). Bogeys behave like schools of fish: calm ones ignore the player and never fire. Coming within 320 units agitates one, and it settles again once the player is beyond 650. Hurting a bogey agitates it, and its panic spreads to schoolmates within 220 units. Below 40% health it goes berserk, which means faster flight, no retreating, rapid fire, and pursuit out to 1500 units. Agitated bogeys strafe at range and fire aimed shots, and the visual cue is color (calm blue, agitated pale, berserk red). These behaviors are genes: the other HOME species are sight-triggered hunters that notice the player within 1000 units (giving up beyond 1500) and alert neighbors in earshot; Lunatics ram, civilization members lead their pursuit, and Fatsos lumber after the player.
- Flinging is a continuous genome trait (the Lunatic is one point, and negative mass adds to it), keeping the prototype's feel from Koules. Anything that touches one (the player, an enemy, an asteroid) is thrown away at 520 to 1000 units per second along a vector randomly skewed up to about 57 degrees from the contact normal, and the Lunatic recoils the other way. A contact also costs the player 18 hull. The player may exceed top speed after a fling and bleeds the excess off over about a second; flung asteroids slowly shed their excess speed. A short per-creature cooldown keeps one touch from flinging repeatedly.
- Kills persist per sector (keyed by a stable spawn index), asteroids shatter into smaller pieces (with a size cap on generated rocks), and nests are rings of pinned stones sheltering bogeys. Stations (hives, foundries, bastions, depots) belong to civilizations only: they breed members, harvest small rocks into guardians and, when destroyed, pay out by how well they were defended; wild life has no spawn points and reproduces naturally (eggs, live birth). Cord-firing species latch, reel and drag the ship (siphoning if their diet says so), and bonded pairs form moving barriers. Jointed bodies (spines and limbs) are built from the body-plan genes, with a travelling wave for slithering; serpents are what that looks like, not a special case. These new elements are gated on sector parameters that are neutral at HOME, so sector (0, 0) is unchanged; see [UNIVERSE.md](UNIVERSE.md).
- Waves are gone for now, since there is a world to explore instead. Score and lives remain.
- Swept projectile collision avoids fast shots passing through targets. Shields recharge after a quiet interval; hull damage consumes one of three lives, followed by a protected respawn. Restart reuses the same random seed to make tuning comparisons repeatable.
- Projectile and effect counts are capped; entity counts are bounded by the loaded sectors. The desktop build uses only 2D rendering, VSync, and WGPU's low-power preference. On macOS it explicitly selects Metal; there is no separate raw-WGPU renderer to maintain.

## Where to iterate

`src/simulation.rs` owns gameplay data, tuning, sector streaming, enemy steering, collision rules, persistence, shattering and scenario tests. Its child modules own the generic creature (`simulation/creature.rs`: steering and weapons), station types, inhabited husks, grazing and broods (`simulation/ecology.rs`), energy and plankton (`simulation/food.rs`), growth, eggs and reproduction (`simulation/growth.rs`), tethers (`simulation/tether.rs`) and jointed bodies (`simulation/chain.rs`), the composable ship augmentation model (`simulation/upgrades.rs`: effects, parts, charges, rolls, stats), the arsenal's owned profiles and boosts (`simulation/arsenal.rs`) and their play: switching, billing, dry fall-back, boost drain (`simulation/arms.rs`), drops, pickups and the loadout in play (`simulation/loot.rs`), the cargo hold, ore, the mining beam and its persistence (`simulation/mining.rs`), economy and ability numbers (`simulation/tuning.rs`), the bench's rig upgrades and the locked parry and dash (`simulation/skills.rs`, `parry.rs`, `dash.rs`), field repair, landing pads, hiding, the bench, pad respawn and the enemy counter (`simulation/pads.rs`), the run record and species extirpation (`simulation/run.rs`, shown by the summary panel in `src/presentation.rs`), and projectile patterns, missile interception, mines and ship secondaries (`simulation/weapons.rs`). `src/genome.rs` owns the genome, species, gene pools, names and colors. `src/range.rs` owns species ranges and the ecology and rock fields, `src/region.rs` region names (announced by `simulation/regions.rs`). `src/world.rs` owns sector coordinates, the latent parameter space, the generation policy, phenotypes, and the deterministic RNG. `src/main.rs` adapts Bevy input and fixed timing to that simulation, and owns the follow camera and window controls. `src/backdrop.rs` computes the sky's character (nebula palette, grain, star density) as a pure function of position and `src/nebula.rs` draws it; `src/presentation.rs` draws the world (starfield, borders, bodies, guides, radar) and the on-demand panels; `src/hud.rs` draws the geometric HUD (rings, pips, bars, prompt, lure) from the library's view-model (`simulation/hud.rs`, with `feel.rs`, `interact.rs` and `lure.rs`); `src/juice.rs` turns feel events into shake and floating marks; `src/settings.rs` is the settings screen. None of them changes game rules. Sound is a one-way pipeline: the simulation appends `Cue`s (`simulation/cues.rs`: shots, impacts, explosions, pickups, damage to the ship), `src/mixer.rs` decides which are heard (distance falloff, pan, per-frame coalescing, per-sound rate limits, a priority-ordered voice budget), `src/synth.rs` bakes procedural samples once at startup, and `src/audio.rs` plays them through `bevy_audio`. Mixer and synth are headless and tested; sound, the backdrop's effects, edge arrows, the camera and the render style are toggled on the settings screen (`Esc`), `X` pings, `M` holds the mining beam and `E` is the one context key (see the README's table). `Cargo.toml` exposes a default `desktop` feature; disabling it leaves the simulation testable without window or GPU initialization.

For a bounded desktop renderer check, set `SSC_SMOKE_FRAMES=120` and run the game. Optionally set `SSC_SCREENSHOT=/tmp/ssc.png` to capture its window before it exits. The screenshot is generated by Bevy, so it does not capture other windows.

- `src/territory.rs` owns the territory lattice, civilization species and their generated spawns; `src/simulation/civ.rs` owns territory tracking, raids, shared doctrine and falls.
- `src/fortress.rs` is the pure fortress layout (archetypes, budgets, flood-fill connectivity); `src/simulation/fortress.rs` plays walls (pinned `RockKind::Wall`) and turrets (`BaseKind::Turret`), and `src/simulation/civmine.rs` holds civilization mining and the capital stash.
- `src/simulation/brain.rs` holds the learner's online-trained predictor (see `docs/UNIVERSE.md`, Learners); it is steered from `creature.rs` (intercept and shot lead) and inherited at the birth sites in `growth.rs` and `ecology.rs`.

## Validation

Headless scenario tests (170+ of them) cover sector math, pure generation, loading and unloading while flying, frozen bodies, chasing across a border, loose emergent crowds, nearby bands merging while distant ones stay independent, alarm spread, combat rules, persistence across unload and reload, rock materials and crystal explosions, nests, inhabited husks, all four destructible station types, mine countdowns, missile interception, dense rail bursts, tethers, chain stability, gene pool smoothness and recurrence, gene extremes and determinism over long flights. Clippy with warnings denied and a native Metal screenshot run also pass. This is not a frame-rate benchmark or a full interactive playtest. CI covers Ubuntu headless tests and a macOS desktop compilation check.

## Current fidelity limits

This is a reimplementation, not a behavior-preserving port. It does not carry over ODE's contact solver, true negative-mass physics (Lunatics only approximate it with a fling rule, above), learned Smarties neural networks, the full set of enemy and weapon-upgrade behaviors, 3D camera effects, original configuration handling, or audio. The original C++ code and assets remain available in git history. These systems can be selectively revisited when they become useful to the new game's direction.

The desktop app currently has procedural visuals and no audio. Build and run instructions are in the [README](../README.md).
