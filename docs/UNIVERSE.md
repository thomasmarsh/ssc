# Universe design

This records the intended shape of the game's procedural universe and how much of it exists today. The design came from a planning discussion; the code is the source of truth for what is built.

## Philosophy

A top-down space shooter with a deterministic, endless universe. Everyone can follow a known route and meet the same places, but no one can see all of it, like navigating a fractal. Quadrant (0, 0) is hand-tuned and familiar. Moving away from it raises danger and strangeness, and uncharted quadrants hold their own surprises.

## Quadrants and the latent space (built)

- The world is an unbounded grid of quadrants (`world::QuadrantId`). A quadrant's contents are a pure function of the master seed and its coordinates, so any quadrant can be unloaded and regenerated identically.
- A quadrant's coordinates are first mapped to a point in a continuous latent space (`world::latent`, `QuadrantParams`): danger, aggression, density, distortion, tech and swarm, each in [0, 1].
  - Distance from the origin sets danger (the exploration curve). The angle around the origin tints flavor: swarms grow on one side, distortion fields on another.
  - Domain-warped fractal noise adds low-frequency variation, so biomes shift gradually between neighbors instead of switching. A test bounds the largest step between adjacent quadrants.
  - Near the origin, parameters ease to `QuadrantParams::HOME`.
- A generation policy (`world::compose`) turns a parameter vector into spawns. It reads only the parameters, so any vector, whether sampled, hand-authored or interpolated, yields a coherent population.
- **Quadrant (0, 0) is HOME.** At HOME every parameter is neutral (0.5, danger 0), and the policy produces exactly the original population. A golden test pins this: counts and a position checksum for three seeds, recorded before the generator was parameterized. The hand-tuned enemies are one point in the space, not special-case code.
- The HUD shows the current quadrant's parameters so the gradient can be felt while flying. `depth` (quadrants from home, unbounded) drives `world::threat`, which toughens and sharpens fauna (see [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md)).

## Gene pools and species (built)

This section was written as the plan and then built as described (`src/genome.rs`). Pipeline: seed -> quadrant -> latent params -> **gene pool** -> compose -> spawns. The code is the source of truth once built; this records the intent.

**Genome** (`genome::Genome`, `Copy`, about 45 genes, each continuous, integer or categorical, each with a range so every genome is finite and bounded):
- Body plan: segment count, limb count and length (a tree of bodies joined by damped springs), joint stiffness, wave strength, rhythm and lag, taper, outline sides and aspect.
- Size and mass: radius, signed mass (negative allowed), hull, shield. Negative mass flips gravity and shot knockback, and makes the body fling whatever touches it.
- Locomotion and sensing: hunt speed, cruise speed, prediction lead, flocking weight, sight and lose ranges, mass affinity, standoff distance, strafe.
- Social structure: solitary, school, pack, brood-tending, dweller (keeps to a home), plus a bond gene (chance to be corded to a sibling).
- Temperament: trigger (sight, proximity or harm), rage threshold, alarm radius.
- Weapons: none, projectile, tether, mines, homing missiles, needle bursts, nova rings or rotating spirals; a volley gene sizes each pattern; fire period, shot speed and range; hardpoint spacing (which parts carry guns); contact fling strength and chaos (continuous, so any creature can be a flinger); ram damage; reel speed.
- Ecology: diet (none, rocks, siphon, dust), fear (none, player, bullets, wells), nest (none, rocks, base).
- Identity: bounty, three name-syllable genes, three pigment genes.

**Species** is a lineage id, a generation and a genome. A lineage is a stable identity (master seed plus the lattice node and slot that founded it) that recurs across quadrants with mutation.

**Gene pools.** Founder lineages live on a coarse lattice (one node every 8 quadrants (`genome::LINEAGE_CELL`, doubled from 4 so new lineages take twice the travel; the biome noise frequency was halved too)). Each node owns a few founders sampled from a seeded distribution over the genome whose bias comes from that node's latent parameters (tech favors chains, guns and keen senses; distortion favors negative mass, waves and odd stiffness; swarm favors schooling; aggression favors rage and flinging; danger favors hull and damage). A quadrant's pool is the founders of the four surrounding nodes, each weighted by bilinear distance, so abundances change smoothly. Each founder is expressed in the quadrant by mutating it with a smooth noise field whose amplitude grows with distance from the founding node, so neighbors hold close relatives of the same lineage and drift is gradual. Categorical genes flip along smooth contours. Sampling uses its own salted stream; the original generation stream is untouched. The existing phenotype stays as the environment's expression layer (quadrant parameters scale flocking, acuity, aggression and mass affinity at runtime).

**How compose uses a pool.** The old slots (school, flingers, hunters, heavies, tether loners and pairs, nests, bases) become niches. Each niche is classified from genes alone (tether weapon, then fling, then heavy mass, then passive schooling, else hunter). A slot picks a pool member of its niche, weighted by abundance; if the pool has none it takes any member, so odd casts appear naturally. The serpent slot disappears: a chain body plan with a wave gene slithers wherever a pool happens to carry one, and an exotic slot spawns random pool members where tech, distortion or danger are high. Counts, spreads and draw order on the original stream do not change.

**How HOME is expressed.** The lattice node at quadrant (0, 0) holds hand-authored founders: five genomes that reproduce Bogey, Lunatic, Smarty, Fatso and Leech. At distance 0 mutation amplitude is zero and only that node has weight, so the HOME pool is exactly those five and the golden test is untouched. Their names and colors are read from their syllable and pigment genes like any species. Moving away, the classics mutate and fade as sampled lineages take over.

**One generic simulation.** Steering, firing, tethers, contacts, joints, breeding, grazing and drawing read the genome (`simulation/creature.rs` and friends). No code branches on a kind, and the renderer draws every body from its body plan and pigment genes. Bodies are capped per quadrant (220 creature bodies) and per creature (28 parts).

**Cut or simplified.** Recombination is within a lineage only (no cross-lineage sex, no speciation). Wave locomotion exists only for jointed bodies. Brood litters stay single-parent. Pools are tuned for weirdness, not fairness: expect quadrants that are unplayable.

## Individual variation (built)

Members of a species are not clones. `Genome::individual` (`src/genome.rs`) varies a genome per creature: about 95% get a tiny jitter (up to 5% of speed, cruise, sight and lose ranges, fire period, radius, flocking, lead, hull, shot speed and range, alarm, standoff and strafe where present, and a shift of pigment), about 4% a wider one (up to 25%), and about 1% also carry one outlier (one gene pushed 2 to 3 times up or down, or social, trigger, fear or diet flipped). The body plan and weapon are never varied, so a species stays recognizable, and everything passes through `limited()`. Randomness comes from its own stream (`INDIVIDUAL_SALT`): generated creatures are varied in a final pass of `compose_with`, keyed by the spawn's stable index, so reloads return the same individual and no other stream moves (the HOME golden test is untouched). Creatures born in play (base broods and guardians, husk tenants, brood litters) are varied at birth from a separate game stream. HOME creatures are varied too; the lineage is unchanged, so compare creatures by lineage, not by whole species.

## Energy, plankton and the food chain (built)

Code: `src/simulation/food.rs` (rules), `world::plankton` (generation), steering hooks in `creature.rs`.

- **Energy.** Every creature body has `energy` and `max_energy` (`Genome::energy_capacity`, 30 + 2.5 x radius). Only foragers (`Genome::forages`: diet rocks, graze or hunt) drain: about 1/600 of capacity per second at rest plus up to 1/900 more with effort, whatever they are doing (combat and rage included). Other diets, base-bred creatures (`provisioned`) and trailing parts stay full. API: `Body::energy_fraction`, `vigor`, `feed(amount)` (returns what was absorbed), `is_starving`.
- **Hunger.** Below 25% energy a forager slows toward 0.6 of its pace (`vigor`). At zero it starves: after 150 seconds it dies, but only while calm (not alert, enraged or panicking) and over 1600 units from the ship, so never in a fight. Death is quiet (`Body::consumed`: no score, no loot, no blast; the spawn stays fallen). Juveniles with a parent, bred creatures and boss-like bodies (hull 150+ or bounty 250+) weaken but never die of hunger. Hull is never touched, so starvation cannot trigger rage.
- **Plankton** (`Game::food`, `Food`) is not a body: it never collides and does not count as a creature. It drifts, swells in over 3 seconds, and is generated per quadrant on its own salted stream (`FOOD_SALT`) as a few blooms, so the original population and the HOME golden test are untouched. The cap per quadrant follows the latent space (`food_richness`: swarm 0.5, calm 0.3, density 0.2; HOME holds about 60) and 60% is present on load. Total plankton is capped at 900 and shares the body budget (`MAX_BODIES`). Plankton is not persisted but is deterministic. Regrowth (`regrow_food`, once a second, on `Game::growth`) is demand-driven, see "Food economy" below.
- **Diets.** `Diet::Graze` eats plankton (sight 650 units) when below 80% energy and steers to it while calm; each speck gives 7 energy. Rock eaters also gain energy from rocks. `Diet::Hunt` is predation: below 60% energy a calm predator chases the nearest lone creature of another lineage lighter than 0.8 of its own mass, and bites on contact (every 2.5 seconds, bypassing shield and never alarming the prey); a kill is eaten whole for about 60% of the prey's capacity. Prey excludes bases, bosses, guardians and base-bred creatures, tended juveniles, jointed bodies, other predators and the ship, and nothing is bitten while fewer than 5 of that lineage remain within 1500 units. Predators treat the ship like anything else.
- **Genes.** The new diets are appended to the diet gene, so old indices hold. HOME Bogeys graze. Sampled wild species draw their foraging diet from one extra final draw (grazers favor swarm, predators aggression and danger, with a minimum mass), so every earlier gene of every sampled species is unchanged.
- **Presentation.** Plankton is drawn as faint pale-lime motes; a forager below 25% energy wears an amber ring that pulses while starving.

## Food economy, rocks and planetoids (built)

Code: `regrow_food`, `sprout_food` and `fertility` in `src/simulation/food.rs`; `world::planetoid`.

- **Demand.** Each hungry grazer (below 80% energy) calls up specks 120 to 520 units from itself at a rate that rises with its hunger and falls to zero as it sees plenty (6 specks plus one per other grazer sharing its sight). A quadrant's output is held to `SUPPLY_RATE` x richness specks a second (its carrying capacity, about 30 grazers per HOME-like quadrant), its standing stock to the cap (half again higher while grazers are hungry), and everything to the global budget. Calm quadrants also seed a speck from nowhere or beside a neighbor, very slowly and only up to the normal cap, so food never fully dies out.
- **Hunger beats hostility.** A grazer below 30% energy breaks off an alert chase to eat (before, an idling ship near a hostile flock starved it). Grazers breed only when they have 3 specks in sight and no more grazers than specks there.
- **Lichen.** Free rocks, ice, ore and nest stones sprout specks beside them (rate by radius; ice 1.6x, ore 0.4x; crystal and husks none), holding only a few nearby. The rock is never edible for it. Rocks wear a few faint lime flecks.
- **Planetoids** (`RockKind::Planetoid`, a pinned asteroid body): fixed, slowly turning, indestructible (`damage` ignores them) and harmless to touch (ships and creatures bounce). Radius 110 to 700, skewed small (a few are vast), on their own stream (`PLANETOID_SALT`), appended as the last spawn so no earlier index moves. At most one per quadrant, never at HOME (golden test intact), chance rising with swarm and calm (`planetoid_chance`), kept 450 units inside the border (so two across a border always leave a channel of 900+) and 220 clear of everything generated (a crowded quadrant gets a smaller world). They bloom plankton continuously in an aura (rate and cap grow with radius), specks slide off the surface, and the oasis draws grazers and predators. No gravity well yet. Drawn with craters, a rim of swaying green tufts and a faint breathing halo.
- **Tests.** HOME idling for 14 minutes keeps its flocks, plankton and energy (seeds 42 and 7), and a wild planetoid quadrant keeps life for 12 minutes under the caps.

## Growth and reproduction (built)

Code: `src/simulation/growth.rs`; genes `Genome::birth` (live or egg) and `Genome::fecundity` (rare, steady, prolific), appended after every older gene and sampled by one extra final draw (HOME Bogeys lay eggs, rarely).

- **Juveniles.** A newborn (`Game::newborn`) wears `Genome::juvenile()` (small, unarmed, one body, schooling) and keeps the genome it will grow into in `Body::adult`, with `age` and `growth` in [0, 1]. Growth takes `35 + adult radius` seconds at full pace and only proceeds with energy: full pace from 60% energy, none at 20% or below (starved juveniles stay small, and are never killed for it: juveniles are exempt from predation and starvation, like tended ones). Radius, mass, hull and energy capacity follow a smooth curve, so they visibly swell. On maturity the adult genome replaces the juvenile one (weapons, shield and social role arrive; a jointed adult grows its whole body, so its head gets a new id), tending parents are left behind and a pale pulse (`EffectKind::Mature`) plays. Brood litters now mature too.
- **Reproduction.** Adult foragers (not provisioned, not brood-tenders, who keep their litters) try every so often (about 75, 150 or 300 seconds by `fecundity`, first try spread by identity) and succeed only when energy is at least 80%, it is calm (the parent and everything within 1400 units is not alert, enraged or panicking), the ship is not within 260 units, food is near (3 plankton, a small rock, or 5 other prey) and nothing is capped. A birth costs 30% of the parent's capacity (a litter 12%); the newborn starts at 70% of its own. Live birth places a juvenile beside the parent; an egg (`Game::eggs`, `Egg`) drifts, can be shot by the ship, hatches after `20 + 0.6 x adult radius` seconds when calm and uncrowded, and spoils after three incubations if it cannot. Offspring get `parent.genome.individual(...)` (generation + 1), and 3% flip the birth mode.
- **Caps.** Per lineage, 12 creatures and eggs within 1300 units; per quadrant, the 220-body creature budget; globally `MAX_BODIES` (eggs and plankton count against it). Eggs unload with their quadrant. Together with food and calm requirements this damps boom and bust: HOME's flocks are already past the local cap, so they only replace losses. A long headless test bounds HOME over twelve minutes.

## Learners (built)

A creature with a `learner` gene above zero (and `learn_rate`, both appended as a trailing gene block so no earlier gene index or draw moved) carries a tiny brain (`simulation/brain.rs`): a 6-6-2 tanh MLP, plain f32, one per creature, boxed in `Body::brain` (`None` for everyone else, so a non-learner pays one pointer and behaves exactly as before). While the creature is hunting, every `INTERVAL` (0.25 s) it snapshots the ship's relative position, velocity and recent acceleration; `HORIZON` (0.5 s) later it takes one SGD step on the difference between where the ship went and its straight-line extrapolation. The net therefore predicts a residual (squashed by tanh and scaled by `RANGE` 240), starts near zero (no worse than a plain lead) and learns to bend the aim around circle-strafes and zigzags within roughly 5 to 20 s. Intercept steering aims at `ship + velocity * lerp(lead, HORIZON, learner) + residual * learner`; weapons lead shots by flight time (`shot_offset`), blended by `learner` (non-learners never lead shots). Every lead is clamped to `MAX_LEAD` (420) so a trained brain is dangerous, not an aimbot. Brains are inherited: at `try_breed`, `tend_broods` and egg hatching the child's weights are a random blend of both parents' (or a copy of one) plus 0.01 noise (`Brain::inherit`, via `Game::inherited_brain`, drawing from the variation stream only for learners). HOME's Smarty is a modest learner (0.6) though none spawn in the opening population; wild gene pools sample learners at about 4% rising with tech. Presentation shows a faint scanning arc that lengthens and brightens (with a glint) as `Body::learner_skill` rises. Knobs: `INTERVAL`, `HORIZON`, `RANGE`, `MAX_LEAD`, `step_size` (0.05 + 0.6 * learn_rate), `INHERIT_NOISE`.

## Phenotypes (built)

The genome is heritable; the phenotype (`world::Phenotype`) is the environment's expression of it, set by the quadrant's parameters and neutral at HOME: `flocking` (reach and strength of schooling), `sensor_acuity` (detection range and prediction lead), `aggression` (pace, fire rate, how readily a creature enrages) and `mass_affinity` (added to the gene).

## Creatures (built)

There are no creature kinds. Everything below is a point in genome space, and the HOME pool reproduces it exactly:

- **Bogey**: proximity trigger, passive until approached or hurt, rage below 40% health, projectile weapon, standoff and strafe, schools.
- **Lunatic**: fling gene 1.0 with full chaos. Anything touching it is thrown at 520 to 1000 units per second (scaled by the gene) along a skewed vector.
- **Smarty**: prediction lead 0.55, fast. **Fatso**: heavy and slow, 200 mass.
- **Leech**: tether weapon, siphoning diet, bond gene 0.6 (so it often comes in corded pairs).
- **Serpent**: the old segmented creature is `Genome::serpent()`, a point reached by chain length plus a wave gene. It is not placed by any code; chains and limbs appear wherever a pool carries them.

Negative mass flips gravity and shot knockback and adds fling strength. Fear (player, bullets, wells), diet (rocks, siphon, dust), brood-tending and dwelling are genes any species can carry.

## Persistence (built)

Every spawn has a stable index within its quadrant (its position in the generator's output). The simulation records which spawns have been destroyed, so a quadrant that unloads and reloads comes back as the player left it: killed creatures, shot-out nest stones, consumed rocks and destroyed bases stay gone. A chain counts as destroyed only when its last segment dies. A spawn whose creature wandered off but is still loaded is not duplicated when its home quadrant reloads. Only kills persist. Positions, damage, bred creatures and shards are not remembered, so the rest of a quadrant regenerates fresh, and a partly destroyed chain returns whole.

## Asteroid structure (built)

- No rock grows past `ASTEROID_MAX_RADIUS` (60), so no single stone can wall off the player. A test checks every generated rock across the explored universe.
- A destroyed rock shatters into two or three pieces (ice adds another), each about 0.62 of its radius, flying apart; pieces under 13 units simply vanish, so shattering always terminates. Rocks also take damage when flung by a Lunatic or when two rocks collide fast.
- **Nests** are rings of nine pinned stones with one stone missing. The opening is wide enough for a ship and the rest of the ring is not, so the hollow is a refuge. A few bogeys graze inside. Stones are ordinary rocks that never move, so shooting or flinging stones into the ring opens new gaps. Nests are more common away from HOME, especially where swarm is high.
- Free rocks vary in outline and material: ice is brittle and yields shield charge; ore is dense and tough with richer salvage; purple crystal bursts on destruction, hurting nearby ships and fauna, and often yields a charge (a weapon unlock or a boost). HOME rocks remain ordinary.
- Inhabited husks have a hollow mouth and moving feelers. They release two to four creatures when approached or shot, including on a lethal hit. Shells and sheltered nests are seeded more frequently away from HOME.

## Mining and materials (built)

The ship carries three materials, `Cargo { metal, volatiles, crystal }`, cap 200 each (`src/simulation/mining.rs`). Hold `M` (gamepad right trigger R2; the bumpers switch weapon) to extend a beam at the nearest minable rock within 260 units of the ship's center to its surface; there is no aiming and no cone. Mining and firing exclude each other (the beam wins while held). The beam draws 4 shield per second, suppresses shield recharge while on, refuses below 6 shield and has no heat. Mute moved to `N`.

- Every rock holds ore = radius^2 / 40 (ore x1.5, husk x0.5), derived from size and kind, never drawn from RNG and never stored with the spawn. Yield per beam second: ore 1.0 metal, plain 0.4 metal, ice 1.0 volatiles, husk shell 0.25 volatiles, planetoid 0.3 of one material picked by a hash of its spawn index. Crystal is harvested in 0.5 s cycles of 4 shards and bursts (the existing crystal blast, ship included) if the beam is held past three cycles; releasing resets the count.
- Mining shrinks the radius as sqrt(ore / full) down to 14, where the rock crumbles into a pickup of its leftover and vanishes (recorded in `fallen`). It never shatters, so mining never multiplies rocks; shatter fragments from shooting split the parent's remaining ore, so shooting cannot create ore. Partial depletion persists in `mined` (by spawn, quantized upward) and is re-applied when a quadrant reloads; rocks without a spawn index are not persisted. Planetoids never shrink and give a 400 unit budget (tracked in `mined`), with no regrowth.
- Tenants: mining a host counts as harm (rooted creatures turn alert for a few seconds); a rock crumbled from under them releases them (`release_from`). Mining a husk wakes its tenants, as an attack does; a hatched husk is plain rock. Pinned nest stones are not minable, so a nest's refuge cannot be eroded (shooting still opens it).
- Dying costs 25% of each material, dropped as pickups where the ship died. Salvage is `Item::Material(kind, amount)`: it adds to the hold, and scores about its amount; a part scrapped for a full slot yields metal (rating x 10) as well as score.

## Field repair, landing pads and the bench (built)

`src/simulation/pads.rs`. Materials now have sinks beyond ammo. Keys: `R` field repair (gamepad X), `K` craft a pad kit (d-pad up), `L` deploy / land / lift off (gamepad B), `E` open the bench while landed (Select), `I` toggle part insurance (d-pad down). The radar toggle moved from `R` to `Tab`.

- **Field repair.** Hull 5/s for 2 metal per 10 hull, then shield 6/s for 2 volatiles per 15 shield. Slow on purpose; any damage stops it and it cannot start within a second of a hit.
- **Kit and pad.** A kit costs 40 metal + 10 crystal (at most 2 carried). Deploy on a planetoid within 120 of its surface at speed below 60; one pad per planetoid, at most 6 live (the oldest is dismantled for a 50% kit refund plus its stash, spill goes to pickups). A pad has 200 hp and a stash (100 per material). Pads are keyed `(QuadrantId, spawn index)` in `Game::pad`, with the anchor in the planetoid's own frame, so they survive unloading and re-attach when the quadrant reloads (no generation change; HOME is untouched).
- **Landing.** Within 80 of a pad below speed 80; refused if a hostile rooted creature (armed rooter or alert) is within 150. The ship is rooted to the planetoid (zero velocity, turns with it). Thrust or `L` lifts off with a 140 impulse. Landed and unseen: hull +8/s, shield +20/s, and creatures notice the ship at 3x the distance (the perceived distance is tripled; hysteresis uses sight, not lose). After 4 s hidden, alert creatures lose the ship unless harmed. Firing (or any shot or mine laid), damage, or a hostile tenant on the planetoid breaks cover for 10 s; no mending happens while exposed, so a pad is not a place to tank. Mining the planetoid does not break cover. Brains get no "player landed" input (a landed ship simply has zero velocity, which they already see).
- **Bench.** Tabs 1 repair (instant, same prices), 2 reforge (6 crystal + 20 metal times rarity index 1-4; rerolls the affixes, best of three, never worse), 3 upgrade (one rarity step: 10C+30M from common, doubling per step, Epic cap; positive stats scale by the strength ratio, penalties stay, one more affix), 4 arms (raise an owned weapon profile: 40 of its material doubling per level plus crystal, up to the trait cap), 5 stash (store or take 25). `[ ]` pick a target, `F` do it, `Q` take. Reforge and upgrade draw from the loot stream. The ship has no spare-part inventory, so "loose" parts are the fitted ones.
- **Respawn.** A death still costs a life, but with a pad the ship returns just above the nearest pad by quadrant distance (ties by key order), streaming that quadrant first. Insurance (default on) pays 10 metal before the death to keep the best part instead of dropping it. Items dropped at the wreck unload if the pad is far. A destroyed pad drops its stash as pickups and respawn falls back to the next pad or the death position. Game over and restart reset pads and cargo.
- **The counter.** Learners and civilization members that come within sight of a pad remember it (per territory for civs); those within 1500 hunt it, and within 300 they gnaw 4 hp/s each (up to 5), hurting a landed ship too. A raid from a territory that knows a pad in the simulated region arrives around the pad. Unloaded quadrants simulate nothing: a pad known to enemies takes one seeded raid roll per reload (hash of seed, quadrant, index and reload count; 50% for 60-220 damage).

## Ecosystem bases (built)

Bases appear where danger, aggression, tech or swarm are high, and never at HOME. Their kind and armament come from a separate seeded stream:

- **Hive:** six organic brood chambers; 450 hull; breeds the local species and harvests small rocks into guardians.
- **Foundry:** square furnace with intake arms; 650 hull; reaches farther for rocks, earns more stock and supports more guardians, with a slower brood.
- **Bastion:** armored octagon with four turret mounts; 800 hull; no brood, aimed volleys, missiles, needles, rings or spirals.
- **Depot:** radial mine magazines and ring emitter; 550 hull; seeds mines, pulses nova rings and breeds slowly.

All stations can be destroyed by ship weapons. Depth toughens their hull more gently than creatures (square root of threat). A hull bar shows progress. Destruction scores 500, guarantees a part with two additional loot rolls, sends nearby creatures into disarray for ten seconds and persists across unload and reload.

## Weapon patterns (built)

Stock pellets and tethers remain. Creatures and station turrets express their weapon and volley genes through the same pattern code. Missiles track the ship, burst on impact or expiry and can be shot down. Rail-style needle bursts contain 48 to 128 fast, weak particles (up to 160 through mutation); nova rings expand in every direction; spirals rotate between rapid discharges. Global projectile and mine caps bound these patterns.

Mines do no contact damage. Proximity arms a visible countdown with a blast-radius warning; leaving before detonation avoids damage. Shooting a hostile mine sets it off early. Friendly mines target enemies and spare the ship.

Ship parts and charges offer missile pods, needle cannons, mine layers and nova emitters as weapon profiles in the arsenal (owned for the run, fuelled from the cargo hold; see ROADMAP, Progression). Rotating spiral emitters and tether launchers remain enemy-only. Projectiles and creature hardpoints have distinct visual cues for their weapons.

## Tethers (built)

A cord thrower fires a cord that flies at the ship. On a hit it latches, reels in (reel speed is a gene) down to a minimum rest length (150, or its own radius plus the ship's plus 80 if larger, so a cord never holds the ship against its owner), pulls the ship with a spring, and, if its diet is siphoning, feeds on shield. A learner leads its tether shot with its brain. Losing a life cuts every latch. Bonded creatures, which the bond gene cords in pairs and clusters, hold a 300 unit cord that pulls them together if stretched; flying through it costs 14 shield and shoves the ship away. A linked cord takes three hits to cut and dies with either end.

**Cord strength is variable** (four appended tail genes, used only by tether weapons): `cord_strength` (1 to 8; pull stiffness and ceiling, 2 per unit stretch times strength, capped at 450 x (1 + strength)), `cord_slack` (200 to 3000; stretch past rest length at which the cord snaps), `cord_hardness` (1 to 10; hits to cut, 15 health per unit, a bullet does 16) and `cord_drag` (0 to 1; share of the ship's speed away from the owner bled off, at up to 4 per second). The defaults (1, 200, 2, 0) are the classic weak cord and HOME's Leech keeps them (individual variation jitters them gently, from already-drawn values so no draws move). Wild sampling (one extra final draw): about 60 percent of cord throwers stay weak, about 28 percent middling (strength 1.2 to 2.2, slack 220 to 330), about 9 percent strong (3 to 5.5, slack 500 to 1200, drag to 0.5), and about 3 percent grip (6 to 8, slack 2000 to 3000, drag 0.5 to 0.9) which the ship cannot out-thrust: shoot it, shear it or kill the owner. Danger and tech nudge the strong share up. Depth scales a cord gently: strength by 15 percent and hardness by 10 percent per point of threat above 1. Strong cords also settle the ship at the rest length instead of slinging it into the owner. Crossover passes the cord genes with the weapon; mutation drifts them for tetherers.

**Fairness for rooted owners.** A cord from a rooted creature (an immovable anchor) is capped at strength 4, hardness 4 (so at most four hits) and drag 0.5, and bleeds the ship's speed toward the anchor so it is not slung into it. Siphoning and contact damage follow the existing rules, so shooting the cord always works and a latched ship never loses shield faster than before.

**Counters.** Shooting always works. Cord shears cut a cord of 45 health or less (weak and middling) the moment it latches and wear a stouter one through at 60 health per second, so they scale with hardness.

**Readability.** Cords draw with one, two (strength 3+) or three (6+) strands, shift from violet toward hot magenta and brighten (blooming in the glow styles), tremble and swell a tension ring with load, redden near snapping, flicker when frayed and wear a clamp at the ship. A latch plays its own heavy twang (`Cue::Latch`, louder for strong cords) and the HUD shows `TETHERED` or `GRIPPED` with a tension gauge.

## Modular bodies (built)

A jointed creature is a tree of ordinary bodies joined by damped springs, built from the body-plan genes: spine length, taper, limb count and length, joint stiffness, wave strength, rhythm and lag. Only the head steers, with extra authority scaled by size so it can tow its body. A sine wave runs down the spine as a sideways acceleration (limbs paddle against it), which turns a straight pull into slithering. Joints are hard-limited to 1.6 times their rest length after movement and contacts, so violent impacts cannot tear a creature apart; tests cover stiffness from 30 to 900 and extreme mass and speed. Parts do not collide with their own creature. Hardpoints (every n-th part, else the head) carry the creature's weapon. Destroyed parts close ranks and a dead head promotes the next part. Not built: rigid or magnetic joint types and hardpoints derived from surface area.

## Rooted life

Any species can carry a habit, five appended tail genes in `src/genome.rs`: `root` (below 0.4 free, up to 0.75 rooted while young, above that rooted for life; a body with a spine can only be rooted while young, see `Genome::habit`), `root_defense` (contact sting multiplier, and young that cling keep their weapon from 0.4), and the detach genes `detach_size` (growth share at which a young one lets go), `detach_hunger` (energy fraction below which it lets go at a poor host) and `detach_crowd` (share of the host rim above which the surplus leave). Wild sampling gives a minority of species a habit (about 7 to 26 percent, more where swarm is high and danger low, one extra final draw so earlier genes stay put); HOME species and HOME rocks are free and ordinary. Crossover passes the whole habit from the body-plan parent; mutation drifts the defense and detach genes of rooters only.

A rooted creature (`Body::root`, `simulation/root.rs`) sits at an anchor angle in its host's frame, so it rides a drifting rock and a turning planetoid, never steers, fires only into the open half-plane (never through its own rock), stings harder on contact, and can be killed, scored and looted like anything else. Hosts are free plain, ice or ore rocks and planetoids (never crystal, husk or nest stones). It feeds from the host's lichen (shared among everyone on it) plus any plankton that drifts by, and grows like any juvenile. A rooted-young habit lets go at maturity (or earlier by the detach genes) and goes free; a lifelong rooter lets go only when its host is destroyed, eaten or unloaded, when it falls free, dazed, and looks for another rock. Eggs and live young of rooting mothers are laid or born onto a rock near the mother and hatch rooted. Rooters are not prey while clinging.

Generation (`world::root_residents`, own salted stream `ROOT_SALT`, appended after everything else): every planetoid with creature budget left gets a community scaled to its radius (one per 26 units, 3 to 36), of native rooters that fit, else one to three sessile cousins of pool species (`Species::sessile`: spineless, own lineage); ordinary rocks sometimes get one or two native rooters. Rooted spawns carry `Spawn::rooted` (host spawn index, anchor, growth); a fallen host spawns its tenants free. Each counts one body against the quadrant budget.

## Civilizations (built)

Code: `src/territory.rs` (where and what), `src/simulation/civ.rs` (play). The learners of the previous section become the real inhabitants: rare territories held by one learner lineage, while everything else stays wildlife.

- **Where.** `world::territory(seed, quadrant) -> Option<Territory>` is pure. Territories live on their own lattice (`TERRITORY_CELL` 10 quadrants, one per cell with chance `TERRITORY_CHANCE` 0.34, own stream `TERRITORY_SALT`): a capital quadrant near the cell middle and a ragged noise-edged disc of radius 2 to 3 quadrants around it (a few quadrants across, about 5 percent of the map). Members are never within `TERRITORY_MIN_DEPTH` (4) quadrants of HOME, so the opening and the HOME golden test are untouched and the nearest territories sit at depth 6 to 8 (threat 2.8 to 3.4).
- **Who.** `Territory` holds an id (also the members' lineage), the capital, a strength (0.7 to 1.4) and a shape: horde, elder or both (title HORDE, COURT, DOMINION). The member species is a pool species of the capital quadrant turned into a learner (learner 0.75 to 1, brisk learn rate, pack, graze diet, projectile gun, 25 to 45 hull), so its name and color come from its genes. Warriors are armored escorts (their own lineage); the elder is a boss (hull 300 and twice that in play, shield 45, bounty 400, learner 1, heavy weapon, standoff and strafe, hunt diet, never breeds, never starves). All are single bodies.
- **What is generated.** Appended last on the quadrant's output from the territory stream (`civ_spawns`; `Spawn::civ`): the capital quadrant holds a base (hive or foundry for hordes, bastion or depot for courts, any for dominions) that breeds members and builds warrior guardians through the existing base code, a garrison, and the elder when the shape has one. Other member quadrants get outposts (smaller bases with members, more often near the capital) and patrols. Creatures are leashed to where they were placed. Everything counts against the 220-body quadrant budget.
- **Behavior.** Inside their own territory members see and give up on the ship 30 percent further, and an alerted member rallies any comrade within 1600 units (only while the ship is near, so alarms cannot sustain themselves). Wildlife edges away from civil creatures. Warriors and members graze plankton; breeding, growth and learning use the usual systems (brains inherited, same-lineage crossover, lineage cap 12). Warriors and the elder are not prey; the capital base harvests rocks (existing base rules).
- **Raids.** While the ship lingers in a living territory a clock runs (`WAR_AT` 60 s, `RAID_AT` 180 s, then every `RAID_EVERY` 120 s): patrol, a war party arriving off screen and hunting the ship, then big raids (double size, the elder joins the call). Party size is `PARTY_BASE` 3 plus 2 x strength, at most `CIV_CAP` 24 alive per civilization. Leaving for `RAID_GRACE` (10 s) resets the clock. Called members answer within 2400 units.
- **Doctrine.** Every 1.5 s the trained brains of a territory are blended into a per-territory table and back (`Brain::blend_toward`), and members created later (raiders, reloaded garrisons) start with the table's weights. The table lives in the `Game`, so it survives quadrants unloading; it is lost on restart (and is not saved, nothing is).
- **Falls.** Destroying the capital base or killing the elder is a lasting flag per territory (`Game::civ_fall`, beside the destroyed-spawn memory). A horde ends when its capital falls, a court when its elder dies, a dominion needs both; one half only weakens (war parties are halved, no big raid, threat verdict lower). A fallen civilization's garrison and bases do not return on reload, it sends no raids, and the territory reports FALLEN. The elder always drops two parts (one epic, one at least rare, graded 25 percent above the place through `Source::min_rarity`) and two lucky rolls, and pays a score bonus; raided elders do not exist (raids never include one).
- **Player-facing.** Entering or leaving posts a notice with the territory name, its threat and the usual SHIP POWER verdict; the HUD shows a line with name, threat, verdict, raid clock and WEAKENED; radar members and bases get a ring in the territory tint, and in-world civil creatures wear a faint banner ring. No audio beyond the existing warp-in cue on raid arrival.
- **Tuning knobs.** `TERRITORY_CHANCE`, `TERRITORY_CELL`, `TERRITORY_MIN_DEPTH`, shape weights and radius range in `territory.rs`; garrison sizes (`share`) and genome clamps in `Territory::member`, `warrior`, `elder`; `Territory::menace` (HUD threat multiplier); `WAR_AT`, `RAID_AT`, `RAID_EVERY`, `RAID_GRACE`, `PARTY_BASE`, `CIV_CAP`, `DOMAIN_SIGHT`, `COORD_RANGE`, `CALL_RANGE`, `SHOO_RANGE`, `TABLE_PULL`, `MEMBER_PULL`, `ELDER_HULL`, `ELDER_SCORE` in `civ.rs`.
- **Known gaps.** Balance is by numbers, not playtested: a bare ship is outclassed in the nearest territories (depth 6 to 8). Doctrine and falls are not persisted past the session. Wildlife is only edged away, never hunted by soldiers. No border cue in the world and no dedicated sounds.

## Not built yet

- **Richer joint graphs.** Other joint types, and weapons derived from surface area.
- **Recombination across lineages.** Same-lineage mating is built: a breeder with a calm, fed adult of its lineage within `MATE_RANGE` (400) crosses the two genomes (`Genome::crossover`, body plan, weapon, temperament and ecology each from one parent, other genes blended) and they split the birth cost; with no mate it clones with `Genome::mutate` (mild: 1% jitter, rare 8%, categories almost never flip). Birth sites are `try_breed` and `find_mate` (`simulation/growth.rs`) and `tend_broods` (`simulation/ecology.rs`). Not built: mating between lineages, mate choice by traits, speciation.
- **Richer social structure.** Territories exist (see Civilizations), but there are no trade, diplomacy, migration between territories or rival civilizations.
- **Richer tethers.** Several creatures linked into larger structures, and cords that wrap on obstacles.
- **Richer persistence.** Positions, damage and bred creatures across unloads.
- **Compute offload.** GPU compute for dense generation and constraint solving. Nothing has been slow enough to justify it: a one-minute flight through a dense area (about 470 bodies, three chains, bases and tethers) averages 0.3 ms per tick in the development profile and 0.15 ms in release. The worst tick, about 13 ms in development, is a quadrant being generated; if that is ever felt, spreading generation across ticks is the first fix.

## Open question

The planning notes describe quadrants of 1200 by 1200 units. They are 6000 by 6000 today (`world::QUADRANT_SIZE`), several screens across. The generation policy scales counts by parameter rather than by area, so changing the size means retuning densities. Which scale feels right is worth deciding by playing.
