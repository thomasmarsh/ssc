# Universe design

This records the intended shape of the game's procedural universe and how much of it exists today. The design came from a planning discussion; the code is the source of truth for what is built.

Status: BUILT - sectors and latent space, gene pools, niches, ecology and growth, learners (civilizations only), creatures, persistence of kills, asteroid structure, mining and materials, the rig, pads and the bench, stations, weapon patterns, tethers, modular bodies, rooted life, civilizations, diplomacy, apex elders, fortified cities, sector map, rare powers and dynamic wells, realms, sniping counters, the backdrop, run stats, HUD and feel, kinetic impacts, charting, legacy and discovery (each section is tagged "built"). PARTIAL - persistence (kills only), realm effects (civilizations untouched). TODO items are in "Not built yet" and inline tagged `TODO:`; sector size (6000 versus the old 1200) is an open question to settle by playing. Bounded screenshot hooks are in [HOOKS.md](HOOKS.md).

## Philosophy

A top-down space shooter with a deterministic, endless universe. Everyone can follow a known route and meet the same places, but no one can see all of it, like navigating a fractal. Sector (0, 0) is hand-tuned and familiar. Moving away from it raises danger and strangeness, and uncharted sectors hold their own surprises.

## Sectors and the latent space (built)

- The world is an unbounded grid of sectors (`world::SectorId`). A sector's contents are a pure function of the master seed and its coordinates, so any sector can be unloaded and regenerated identically.
- A sector's coordinates are first mapped to a point in a continuous latent space (`world::latent`, `SectorParams`): danger, aggression, density, distortion, tech and swarm, each in [0, 1].
  - Distance from the origin sets danger (the exploration curve). The angle around the origin tints flavor: swarms grow on one side, distortion fields on another.
  - Domain-warped fractal noise adds low-frequency variation, so biomes shift gradually between neighbors instead of switching. A test bounds the largest step between adjacent sectors.
  - Near the origin, parameters ease to `SectorParams::HOME`.
- A generation policy (`world::compose`) turns a parameter vector into spawns. It reads only the parameters, so any vector, whether sampled, hand-authored or interpolated, yields a coherent population.
- **Sector (0, 0) is HOME.** Every parameter is neutral there (0.5, danger 0). HOME is a peaceful start (see "Niches and the ecology field"): rocks, plankton and a planetoid, no creatures. A golden test pins its counts and a position checksum for three seeds; it replaced the original C++ population in a deliberate clean break.
- The details panel (hold Tab) shows the current sector's parameters so the gradient can be felt while flying; the always-visible HUD shows only the threat pips (see HUD, input and feel). `depth` (sectors from home, unbounded) drives `world::threat`, which toughens and sharpens fauna (see [ROADMAP.md](ROADMAP.md)).

## Gene pools and species (built)

This section was written as the plan and then built as described (`src/genome.rs`). Pipeline: seed -> sector -> latent params -> **gene pool** -> compose -> spawns. The code is the source of truth once built; this records the intent.

**Genome** (`genome::Genome`, `Copy`, about 45 genes, each continuous, integer or categorical, each with a range so every genome is finite and bounded):
- Body plan: segment count, limb count and length (a tree of bodies joined by damped springs), joint stiffness, wave strength, rhythm and lag, taper, outline sides and aspect.
- Size and mass: radius, signed mass (negative allowed), hull, shield. Negative mass flips gravity and shot knockback, and makes the body fling whatever touches it.
- Locomotion and sensing: hunt speed, cruise speed, prediction lead, flocking weight, sight and lose ranges, mass affinity, standoff distance, strafe.
- Social structure: solitary, school, pack, brood-tending, dweller (keeps to a home), plus a bond gene (chance to be corded to a sibling).
- Temperament: trigger (sight, proximity or harm), rage threshold, alarm radius.
- Weapons: none, projectile, tether, mines, homing missiles, needle bursts, nova rings or rotating spirals; a volley gene sizes each pattern; fire period, shot speed and range; hardpoint spacing (which parts carry guns); contact fling strength and chaos (continuous, so any creature can be a flinger); ram damage; reel speed.
- Ecology: diet (none, rocks, siphon, dust), fear (none, player, bullets, wells), nest (none, rocks, base).
- Identity: bounty, three name-syllable genes, three pigment genes.

**Species** is a lineage id, a generation and a genome. A lineage is a stable identity (master seed plus the lattice node and slot that founded it) that recurs across sectors with mutation.

**Gene pools (superseded for placement by niches, below; genome sampling and expression are unchanged).** Founder lineages lived on a coarse lattice (one node every 8 sectors (`genome::LINEAGE_CELL`, doubled from 4 so new lineages take twice the travel; the biome noise frequency was halved too)). Each node owns a few founders sampled from a seeded distribution over the genome whose bias comes from that node's latent parameters (tech favors chains, guns and keen senses; distortion favors negative mass, waves and odd stiffness; swarm favors schooling; aggression favors rage and flinging; danger favors hull and damage). A sector's pool is the founders of the four surrounding nodes, each weighted by bilinear distance, so abundances change smoothly. Each founder is expressed in the sector by mutating it with a smooth noise field whose amplitude grows with distance from the founding node, so neighbors hold close relatives of the same lineage and drift is gradual. Categorical genes flip along smooth contours. Sampling uses its own salted stream; the original generation stream is untouched. The existing phenotype stays as the environment's expression layer (sector parameters scale flocking, acuity, aggression and mass affinity at runtime).

**How compose uses a pool.** The old slots (school, flingers, hunters, heavies, tether loners and pairs, nests; the old base slot is retired) become niches. Each niche is classified from genes alone (tether weapon, then fling, then heavy mass, then passive schooling, else hunter). A slot picks a pool member of its niche, weighted by abundance; if the pool has none it takes any member, so odd casts appear naturally. The serpent slot disappears: a chain body plan with a wave gene slithers wherever a pool happens to carry one, and an exotic slot spawns random pool members where tech, distortion or danger are high. Counts, spreads and draw order on the original stream do not change.

**How HOME is expressed.** The lattice node at sector (0, 0) holds hand-authored founders: five genomes that reproduce Bogey, Lunatic, Smarty, Fatso and Leech. At distance 0 mutation amplitude is zero and only that node has weight, so the HOME pool is exactly those five and the golden test is untouched. Their names and colors are read from their syllable and pigment genes like any species. Moving away, the classics mutate, thin out over rings 7 to 15 and become rare pockets as sampled lineages take over.

**One generic simulation.** Steering, firing, tethers, contacts, joints, breeding, grazing and drawing read the genome (`simulation/creature.rs` and friends). No code branches on a kind, and the renderer draws every body from its body plan and pigment genes. Bodies are capped per sector (220 creature bodies) and per creature (28 parts).

**Cut or simplified.** Recombination is within a lineage only (no cross-lineage sex, no speciation). Wave locomotion exists only for jointed bodies. Brood litters stay single-parent. Pools are tuned for weirdness, not fairness: expect sectors that are unplayable.

## Niches and the ecology field (built)

Code: `src/range.rs` (niches, belts, matter, ecology), `src/biome.rs` (biomes), `src/region.rs` (names), tuning consts at the top of `range.rs` and `biome.rs`. Replaces the earlier species range blobs (and before them the per-sector independent species picks). A clean break of generation: seeds chart a different universe than before.

- **Abundance of a species at a sector** = depth profile x patch mask x biome affinity x its own fine pockets (`Distribution::abundance_in`).
  - *Depth profile*: a function of the distance from HOME with soft shoulders (`start`, `rise`, `end`, `fall`). Sampled generalists span tens of rings, specialists a few. The four wild classics are the intro: full strength to ring 7 (Fatsos), 9 (Bogeys) or 10 (Lunatics, Leeches), gone five rings later, and after that each lives on only as its **relic** (`Distribution::relic`): endemic-like islands of its own noise (`RELIC_*`) in a couple of percent of sectors, so the far universe is the sampled catalog with the odd old friend. A test measures the classic share of presences by ring band (over half through ring 8, about a fifth over 9 to 12, about 5 percent beyond).
  - *Patch mask*: the species' own low-frequency noise, cut at a threshold chosen so the mask covers a set share of its depth band. One parameter, `breadth`, slides a species from endemic (about 5 to 20 percent, isolated islands 3 to 10 sectors across) through regional (40 to 60 percent, broad patches) to a generalist (80 to 95 percent, with pockets of absence).
  - *Biome affinity*: see Biomes. *Pockets*: a fine noise of about 2 sectors per species.
  - The catalog is endless: depth is cut into tiers of 6 rings and every tier rolls 8 species (7 percent generalist, 30 regional, the rest endemic) from a salted stream; the five classics are fixed entries (`Distribution::classic`). A species' stable key is its lineage (`SpeciesKey`) and `range::regional_noise(seed, key, channel, at, frequency)` is a per-species spatial noise sample: the hook for per-species behaviours over space (the civilization affinities follow-up uses it). The sampled founding genome is pulled toward the character of the species' favourite biome (`FOUNDER_PULL`).
- **Diversity cap.** After the abundances, a sector keeps its strongest K species. K comes from a low-frequency diversity field (`range::diversity`): usually 2 to 4, rarely up to 6, and never more (`MAX_SPECIES`). Ranking is soft (ties rank half way) and the species past K fade, so a border is a slope; weights under 0.03 are dropped, up to 0.12 are eased in. Ring 3 is held to at least 3 (sliding down over the next rings) so the blend begins richly.
- **Ramp by Moore ring from HOME.** Ring 0 HOME: no creatures, no wells, plain rocks and plankton, one planetoid 1700 to 2100 units out (always), and a free pad on it from the first tick (landing, repair and the bench work at spawn; it is not counted against `MAX_PADS`, never dismantled and not a respawn point). Ring 1: Fatsos only. Ring 2: a full ring of Fatsos and Bogeys (every ring-2 sector holds Bogeys; no learners). Ring 3: the blending begins and Lunatics debut there (a fine noise switches them on across ring 3, fading over three more rings). Past that, Lunatics from ring 3, Leeches from 4, sampled species from 3 with jointed or cord-throwing or flinging ones from 4 and predators and heavy weapons from 5 (judged on the founding genome). Nests, husks, exotics, wells and rooted residents wait for ring 3. The start rings are hard rules that bypass the mask, biomes and cap.
- **Biomes** (`biome::biome`). A macro layer of weighted Voronoi cells (one point per 12 sectors, additive weights, edges warped by noise) roughly 8 to 20 sectors across, in eight kinds: open fauna, schooling plains, grazing meadows, predator country, strange lands, keen lands, rough country, hardy lands. Each kind has a character (aggression, swarm, tech, distortion). A species favours one kind and is `picky` about it (generalists little, endemics a lot); affinity falls with the difference between characters, and the four nearest cells share a sector by a soft weight so borders are slopes.
- **Rock belts mask life.** `range::belt` is the ridge of a slow noise field (about 50 sectors across, belts 3 to 4 sectors wide, fading over 3): all abundances are multiplied by `1 - 0.95 x depth`, so belts are quiet mining zones and natural barriers between populations (about 12 percent of the map is deep belt). A planetoid inside a belt, for about 40 percent of those that qualify, is an **oasis**: a small patch (at most 2 species at a quarter of strength) of the sector's own life. `matter` keeps its meaning (ridge noise plus the belt, pushed up where little lives), replacing the `density` parameter; `life` (a saturating sum of the capped abundances) feeds `swarm` (65 percent), so plankton, planetoid chance and flocking follow it. Planetoids are likelier in rich, lush places. `fields()` is the belt's own life and matter; the oasis only restores who lives there (`Ecology::oasis`).
- **Speciation by isolation.** A species' genome in a sector is its founder plus a *cline* (smooth per-gene noise, 20 sectors across, small amplitude) and, for an isolated population, an *offset* hashed from the species and the population's id, scaled by how far the population's heart is from the species' anchor (`ISOLATION_SCALE` 40 sectors). A population is the connected group of mask-lattice vertices around the sector (above the threshold, not cut by a belt, at most 60 vertices; a bigger group is the continent, not offset): `Distribution::patch`. Split groups therefore look more different than connected ones, far ones may nearly be a new species, and the classics stay type specimens on the start rings and drift only a little (`CLINE_AMPLITUDE`, `PATCH_AMPLITUDE`). The machinery is `Genome::drifted`; the original generation stream is untouched.
- **Sanctuary.** While the ship is in HOME no creature goes hostile (nothing hunts, rallies or sieges) unless hurt (`Game::sanctuary`; tests that stage fights at the origin use `empty_game`, which turns it off).
- **Civilizations** use the blob model (`range::blob_reach`). Ordinary territories keep at least depth 6 (and never within ring 2), are capped in strength by depth (`0.45 + 0.1 x depth`) and are accepted by how close the capital's life is to medium-high (0.7) and how rock-rich its neighbourhood is. A seed-fixed early **outpost** (`territory::outpost`, `CivShape::Outpost`) sits 3 to 5 sectors from HOME: a small blob with a hive and a few frail settlers whose trigger is harm only, no soldiers, walls or raids, and a low threat; settlers never learn of a pad. Tuning: `OUTPOST_*`, `TERRITORY_*`, `STRENGTH_*`, `LIFE_TARGET`, `ROCK_WANTED` in `territory.rs`.
- **Regions** (`region::region`): a biome cell names a place (soft syllables for wildlife, harsh ones for civilizations, shared with `Territory::name`, ending in the biome's word such as Shoals or Marches), unless a regional or endemic species is strongly present (0.5+) and then its own population (species and patch) names it. Special forms: `Reach` (sparse gap), `Belt` (deep in a rock belt), `Oasis`, `Confluence` (five or more species at weight 0.35+, rare). The ship announces `ENTERING  NAME` (Epic notice, like the extirpation one) when a new region has held it 3 seconds and 12 seconds have passed since the last banner (`REGION_HOLD`, `REGION_COOLDOWN` in `tuning.rs`). Regions entered count in the run summary, the name shows in the HUD's top line (fading after six seconds to a quiet tag) and on the star map for charted sectors.
- **Persistence.** There is no saved world version: the game keeps nothing on disk, and `fallen` is by spawn index in memory, so a generator change only matters within a session.

## Individual variation (built)

Members of a species are not clones. `Genome::individual` (`src/genome.rs`) varies a genome per creature: about 95% get a tiny jitter (up to 5% of speed, cruise, sight and lose ranges, fire period, radius, flocking, lead, hull, shot speed and range, alarm, standoff and strafe where present, and a shift of pigment), about 4% a wider one (up to 25%), and about 1% also carry one outlier (one gene pushed 2 to 3 times up or down, or social, trigger, fear or diet flipped). The body plan and weapon are never varied, so a species stays recognizable, and everything passes through `limited()`. Randomness comes from its own stream (`INDIVIDUAL_SALT`): generated creatures are varied in a final pass of `compose_with`, keyed by the spawn's stable index, so reloads return the same individual and no other stream moves (the HOME golden test is untouched). Creatures born in play (base broods and guardians, husk tenants, brood litters) are varied at birth from a separate game stream. HOME creatures are varied too; the lineage is unchanged, so compare creatures by lineage, not by whole species.

## Energy, plankton and the food chain (built)

Code: `src/simulation/food.rs` (rules), `world::plankton` (generation), steering hooks in `creature.rs`.

- **Energy.** Every creature body has `energy` and `max_energy` (`Genome::energy_capacity`, 30 + 2.5 x radius). Only foragers (`Genome::forages`: diet rocks, graze or hunt) drain: about 1/600 of capacity per second at rest plus up to 1/900 more with effort, whatever they are doing (combat and rage included). Other diets, base-bred creatures (`provisioned`) and trailing parts stay full. API: `Body::energy_fraction`, `vigor`, `feed(amount)` (returns what was absorbed), `is_starving`.
- **Hunger.** Below 25% energy a forager slows toward 0.6 of its pace (`vigor`). At zero it starves: after 150 seconds it dies, but only while calm (not alert, enraged or panicking) and over 1600 units from the ship, so never in a fight. Death is quiet (`Body::consumed`: no score, no loot, no blast; the spawn stays fallen). Juveniles with a parent, bred creatures and boss-like bodies (hull 150+ or bounty 250+) weaken but never die of hunger. Hull is never touched, so starvation cannot trigger rage.
- **Plankton** (`Game::food`, `Food`) is not a body: it never collides and does not count as a creature. It drifts, swells in over 3 seconds, and is generated per sector on its own salted stream (`FOOD_SALT`) as a few blooms, so the original population and the HOME golden test are untouched. The cap per sector follows the latent space (`food_richness`: swarm 0.5, calm 0.3, density 0.2; HOME holds about 60) and 60% is present on load. Total plankton is capped at 900 and shares the body budget (`MAX_BODIES`). Plankton is not persisted but is deterministic. Regrowth (`regrow_food`, once a second, on `Game::growth`) is demand-driven, see "Food economy" below.
- **Diets.** `Diet::Graze` eats plankton (sight 650 units) when below 80% energy and steers to it while calm; each speck gives 7 energy. Rock eaters also gain energy from rocks. `Diet::Hunt` is predation: below 60% energy a calm predator chases the nearest lone creature of another lineage lighter than 0.8 of its own mass, and bites on contact (every 2.5 seconds, bypassing shield and never alarming the prey); a kill is eaten whole for about 60% of the prey's capacity. Prey excludes bases, bosses, guardians and base-bred creatures, tended juveniles, jointed bodies, other predators and the ship, and nothing is bitten while fewer than 5 of that lineage remain within 1500 units. Predators treat the ship like anything else.
- **Genes.** The new diets are appended to the diet gene, so old indices hold. HOME Bogeys graze. Sampled wild species draw their foraging diet from one extra final draw (grazers favor swarm, predators aggression and danger, with a minimum mass), so every earlier gene of every sampled species is unchanged.
- **Presentation.** Plankton is drawn as faint pale-lime motes; a forager below 25% energy wears an amber ring that pulses while starving.

## Food economy, rocks and planetoids (built)

Code: `regrow_food`, `sprout_food` and `fertility` in `src/simulation/food.rs`; `world::planetoid`.

- **Demand.** Each hungry grazer (below 80% energy) calls up specks 120 to 520 units from itself at a rate that rises with its hunger and falls to zero as it sees plenty (6 specks plus one per other grazer sharing its sight). A sector's output is held to `SUPPLY_RATE` x richness specks a second (its carrying capacity, about 30 grazers per HOME-like sector), its standing stock to the cap (half again higher while grazers are hungry), and everything to the global budget. Calm sectors also seed a speck from nowhere or beside a neighbor, very slowly and only up to the normal cap, so food never fully dies out.
- **Hunger beats hostility.** A grazer below 30% energy breaks off an alert chase to eat (before, an idling ship near a hostile flock starved it). Grazers breed only when they have 3 specks in sight and no more grazers than specks there.
- **Lichen.** Free rocks, ice, ore and nest stones sprout specks beside them (rate by radius; ice 1.6x, ore 0.4x; crystal and husks none), holding only a few nearby. The rock is never edible for it. Rocks wear a few faint lime flecks.
- **Planetoids** (`RockKind::Planetoid`, a pinned asteroid body): fixed, slowly turning, indestructible (`damage` ignores them) and harmless to touch (ships and creatures bounce). Radius 110 to 700, skewed small (a few are vast), on their own stream (`PLANETOID_SALT`), appended as the last spawn so no earlier index moves. At most one per sector, never at HOME (golden test intact), chance rising with swarm and calm (`planetoid_chance`), kept 450 units inside the border (so two across a border always leave a channel of 900+) and 220 clear of everything generated (a crowded sector gets a smaller world). They bloom plankton continuously in an aura (rate and cap grow with radius), specks slide off the surface, and the oasis draws grazers and predators. No gravity well yet. Drawn with craters, a rim of swaying green tufts and a faint breathing halo.
- **Tests.** HOME idling for 14 minutes keeps its flocks, plankton and energy (seeds 42 and 7), and a wild planetoid sector keeps life for 12 minutes under the caps.

## Flora and farming (built, first slice; generator version 27)

Code: `src/flora.rs` (the edibility model and species table), `src/simulation/farm.rs` (plants, planting, harvest, grazing), steering in `creature.rs`, drawing in `presentation.rs` (`draw_plants`).

- **Edibility is a relation.** A plant has a `Chemistry` (sugar, fibre, resin, mineral, toxin, each 0 to 1); a consumer has a `Palate` (a weight per axis, negative for a dislike or poison). Nutrition is the dot product clamped to [0, 1]; at 0.3 (`EDIBLE`) it counts as food. Nothing names a plant or a creature kind. The ship's palate is a constant (`SHIP_PALATE`: fibre and resin make biomass, toxin ruins it, sugar alone is ignored); a creature's palate is a pure function of the master seed and its lineage (`creature_palate`, own salt), always valuing sugar so every grazer eats plankton, the rest drawn per lineage. Plankton is just a chemistry (`PLANKTON`, nearly all sugar): the ship ignores it, every grazer eats it. So a species can be a crop only the ship wants, forage only creatures want, both, or neither (`flora::Role`).
- **Species table.** `flora::species(seed, id)`: 12 species per universe, a chemistry, a `GrammarGenome` shape (the plant L-system of `PROCGEN.md`), a grow time (240 to 600 s), a name and a tint. Slots are drawn to fill a fixed role pattern against a reference crowd of palates, so every universe has crops, shared crops, forage and weeds. Chemistry has `mutate` and `crossover` so crop breeding (workstream 1 slice 3) is the same machinery as the shape genes.
- **Plants** (`Farm::plants`): species, planetoid key, anchor angle in the planetoid's frame, `base` growth and `since` clock. Growth = `base + (time - since) / grow_secs`, clamped, a pure function of the game clock, so unloaded planetoids keep growing and nothing is stepped per plant. A planetoid is stocked with wild plants (own stream, salt `FARM_SALT`; 1 to 6 by radius, three local species) the first time it loads; HOME's planetoid is a tasting menu (a crop only the ship wants, a shared crop, forage), ripe from the start, so the first minutes show the idea.
- **Planting.** The one context key (`interact`): with a seed in hand, within 160 of a planetoid's surface, slower than 60, and 90 units clear of other plants and pads, the key reads `PLANT <NAME>`; it beats DEPLOY PAD and BUILD PAD but not LAND, the bench or a working tithe. The seedling starts at growth 0.05.
- **Harvest** is the mining beam: the nearest ship-edible plant in reach at growth 0.5 or more is cut before the world under it is mined (another rock nearer still wins), after one second of beam. Each cut is a seeded roll (`harvest_roll`, a hash of game seed, plant id and the plant's harvest count, so replays and reloads agree). Ripe (0.9): the full yield (6 times the ship's nutrition) with 85 percent chance, and 0, 1 or 2 seeds of the species (25, 45, 30 percent; 1.05 expected, so replanting is sustainable on average); regrows from a stump at 0.4. Unripe: a quarter-ish yield half the time, one seed 35 percent of the time, and the plant dies (over-harvest kills). Plants the ship cannot use are ignored by the beam. Biomass (cap 60) mends the hull first in a field repair (0.35 a point, cheaper than metal); this is the trivial bench-adjacent sink until machines exist.
- **Grazing.** A hungry creature with the Graze diet eats the plants its palate accepts when it touches them, bites growth down to at most 0.45 (never kills, so a crop is only set back), and gains energy by nutrition; a solitary grazer also steers to a plant it likes (stronger than a planetoid's push-away). Schooling grazers (HOME's Bogeys) do not steer to plants, so the calibrated schools keep their shape, but they graze what they drift past.
- Not yet: seeds from creatures, crop breeding, civilizations that farm, greenroom stations, a HUD pick for which seed, machines using biomass.

## Growth and reproduction (built)

Code: `src/simulation/growth.rs`; genes `Genome::birth` (live or egg) and `Genome::fecundity` (rare, steady, prolific), appended after every older gene and sampled by one extra final draw (HOME Bogeys lay eggs, rarely).

- **Juveniles.** A newborn (`Game::newborn`) wears `Genome::juvenile()` (small, unarmed, one body, schooling) and keeps the genome it will grow into in `Body::adult`, with `age` and `growth` in [0, 1]. Growth takes `35 + adult radius` seconds at full pace and only proceeds with energy: full pace from 60% energy, none at 20% or below (starved juveniles stay small, and are never killed for it: juveniles are exempt from predation and starvation, like tended ones). Radius, mass, hull and energy capacity follow a smooth curve, so they visibly swell. On maturity the adult genome replaces the juvenile one (weapons, shield and social role arrive; a jointed adult grows its whole body, so its head gets a new id), tending parents are left behind and a pale pulse (`EffectKind::Mature`) plays. Brood litters now mature too.
- **Reproduction.** Adult foragers (not provisioned, not brood-tenders, who keep their litters) try every so often (about 75, 150 or 300 seconds by `fecundity`, first try spread by identity) and succeed only when energy is at least 80%, it is calm (the parent and everything within 1400 units is not alert, enraged or panicking), the ship is not within 260 units, food is near (3 plankton, a small rock, or 5 other prey) and nothing is capped. A birth costs 30% of the parent's capacity (a litter 12%); the newborn starts at 70% of its own. Live birth places a juvenile beside the parent; an egg (`Game::eggs`, `Egg`) drifts, can be shot by the ship, hatches after `20 + 0.6 x adult radius` seconds when calm and uncrowded, and spoils after three incubations if it cannot. Offspring get `parent.genome.individual(...)` (generation + 1), and 3% flip the birth mode.
- **Caps.** Per lineage, 12 creatures and eggs within 1300 units and 60 in the whole loaded world (`LINEAGE_WORLD_CAP`: the local gate only looks around the parent, so alone it let a brooding non-forager seed cluster after cluster and fill every sector's budget; the world cap is what bounds a lineage); per sector, the 220-body creature budget; globally `MAX_BODIES` (eggs and plankton count against it). Eggs unload with their sector. Together with food and calm requirements this damps boom and bust: HOME's flocks are already past the local cap, so they only replace losses. A long headless test bounds HOME over twelve minutes.

## Learners (built)

A creature with a `learner` gene above zero (and `learn_rate`, both appended as a trailing gene block so no earlier gene index or draw moved) carries a tiny brain (`simulation/brain.rs`): a 6-6-2 tanh MLP, plain f32, one per creature, boxed in `Body::brain` (`None` for everyone else, so a non-learner pays one pointer and behaves exactly as before). While the creature is hunting, every `INTERVAL` (0.25 s) it snapshots the ship's relative position, velocity and recent acceleration; `HORIZON` (0.5 s) later it takes one SGD step on the difference between where the ship went and its straight-line extrapolation. The net therefore predicts a residual (squashed by tanh and scaled by `RANGE` 240), starts near zero (no worse than a plain lead) and learns to bend the aim around circle-strafes and zigzags within roughly 5 to 20 s. Intercept steering aims at `ship + velocity * lerp(lead, HORIZON, learner) + residual * learner`; weapons lead shots by flight time (`shot_offset`), blended by `learner` (non-learners never lead shots). Every lead is clamped to `MAX_LEAD` (420) so a trained brain is dangerous, not an aimbot. Brains are inherited: at `try_breed`, `tend_broods` and egg hatching the child's weights are a random blend of both parents' (or a copy of one) plus 0.01 noise (`Brain::inherit`, via `Game::inherited_brain`, drawing from the variation stream only for learners). Learners belong to civilizations only: `Genome::sample` still makes its old learning draw (so later genes keep their stream) but discards it, no catalog species or relic pocket is a Smarty (`Family::Smarty` is gone; `Species::smarty` remains as an authored genome for tests), and the apex pack hunter keeps a perfect `lead` gene rather than a brain. The spinning arc therefore marks civilization members and elders and nothing in the wilds. Presentation shows a faint scanning arc that lengthens and brightens (with a glint) as `Body::learner_skill` rises. Knobs: `INTERVAL`, `HORIZON`, `RANGE`, `MAX_LEAD`, `step_size` (0.05 + 0.6 * learn_rate), `INHERIT_NOISE`.

## Phenotypes (built)

The genome is heritable; the phenotype (`world::Phenotype`) is the environment's expression of it, set by the sector's parameters and neutral at HOME: `flocking` (reach and strength of schooling), `sensor_acuity` (detection range and prediction lead), `aggression` (pace, fire rate, how readily a creature enrages) and `mass_affinity` (added to the gene).

## Creatures (built)

There are no creature kinds. Everything below is a point in genome space, and the HOME pool reproduces it exactly:

- **Bogey**: proximity trigger, passive until approached or hurt, rage below 40% health, projectile weapon, standoff and strafe, schools.
- **Lunatic**: fling gene 1.0 with full chaos. Anything touching it is thrown at 520 to 1000 units per second (scaled by the gene) along a skewed vector.
- **Smarty**: prediction lead 0.55, fast. **Fatso**: heavy and slow, 200 mass.
- **Leech**: tether weapon, siphoning diet, bond gene 0.6 (so it often comes in corded pairs).
- **Serpent**: the old segmented creature is `Genome::serpent()`, a point reached by chain length plus a wave gene. It is not placed by any code; chains and limbs appear wherever a pool carries them.

Negative mass flips gravity and shot knockback and adds fling strength. Fear (player, bullets, wells), diet (rocks, siphon, dust), brood-tending and dwelling are genes any species can carry.

## Persistence (built)

Every spawn has a stable index within its sector (its position in the generator's output). The simulation records which spawns have been destroyed, so a sector that unloads and reloads comes back as the player left it: killed creatures, shot-out nest stones, consumed rocks and destroyed bases stay gone. A chain counts as destroyed only when its last segment dies. A spawn whose creature wandered off but is still loaded is not duplicated when its home sector reloads. Only kills persist. Positions, damage, bred creatures and shards are not remembered, so the rest of a sector regenerates fresh, and a partly destroyed chain returns whole.

## Asteroid structure (built)

- No rock grows past `ASTEROID_MAX_RADIUS` (60), so no single stone can wall off the player. A test checks every generated rock across the explored universe.
- A destroyed rock shatters into two or three pieces (ice adds another), each about 0.62 of its radius, flying apart; pieces under 13 units simply vanish, so shattering always terminates. Rocks also take damage when flung by a Lunatic or when two rocks collide fast.
- **Nests** are rings of nine pinned stones with one stone missing. The opening is wide enough for a ship and the rest of the ring is not, so the hollow is a refuge. A few bogeys graze inside. Stones are ordinary rocks that never move, so shooting or flinging stones into the ring opens new gaps. Nests are more common away from HOME, especially where swarm is high.
- Free rocks vary in outline and material: ice is brittle and yields shield charge; ore is dense and tough with richer salvage; purple crystal bursts on destruction, hurting nearby ships and fauna, and often yields a charge (a weapon unlock or a boost). HOME rocks remain ordinary.
- Inhabited husks have a hollow mouth and moving feelers. They release two to four creatures when approached or shot, including on a lethal hit. Shells and sheltered nests are seeded more frequently away from HOME.

## Mining and materials (built)

The ship carries three materials, `Cargo { metal, volatiles, crystal }`, cap 200 each (`src/simulation/mining.rs`). Hold `M` (gamepad right trigger R2; the bumpers switch weapon) to extend a beam at the nearest minable rock within 260 units of the ship's center to its surface; there is no aiming and no cone. Mining and firing exclude each other (the beam wins while held). The beam draws 4 shield per second, suppresses shield recharge while on, refuses below 6 shield and has no heat. Mute moved to `N`.

- Every rock holds ore = radius^2 / 40 (ore x1.5, husk x0.5), derived from size and kind, never drawn from RNG and never stored with the spawn. Yield per beam second: ore 1.0 metal, plain 0.4 metal, ice 1.0 volatiles, husk shell 0.25 volatiles, planetoid 0.3 of one material picked by a hash of its spawn index. Crystal is harvested in 0.5 s cycles of 4 shards and bursts (the existing crystal blast, ship included) if the beam is held past three cycles; releasing resets the count.
- Mining shrinks the radius as sqrt(ore / full) down to 14, where the rock crumbles into a pickup of its leftover and vanishes (recorded in `fallen`). It never shatters, so mining never multiplies rocks; shatter fragments from shooting split half of the parent's remaining ore (`SHOT_ORE_KEEP`), so shooting cannot create ore and loses some. Partial depletion persists in `mined` (by spawn, quantized upward) and is re-applied when a sector reloads; rocks without a spawn index are not persisted. Planetoids never shrink and give a 400 unit budget (tracked in `mined`), with no regrowth.
- Tenants: mining a host counts as harm (rooted creatures turn alert for a few seconds); a rock crumbled from under them releases them (`release_from`). Mining a husk wakes its tenants, as an attack does; a hatched husk is plain rock. Pinned nest stones are not minable, so a nest's refuge cannot be eroded (shooting still opens it).
- Dying costs 25% of each material, dropped as pickups where the ship died. Salvage is `Item::Material(kind, amount)`: it adds to the hold, and scores about its amount; a part scrapped for a full slot yields metal (rating x 10) as well as score.

Balance (all numbers in `src/simulation/tuning.rs`): free rocks take 1/80 of the damage from the ship's own shots, blasts and ram (`ROCK_HULL_FACTOR`, applied by `armored` in `simulation.rs`; collisions and enemy fire still break them, so rock-eating ecology is unchanged, and pinned nest stones, planetoids and walls are exempt), and shooting one surfaces much less (salvage 2 percent, ice 6, ore 5, crystal 12). The beam is the way to material: ore 7 per second, ice 6, plain 4.5, husk 2.5, planetoid 1.2, so a 40 radius ore rock (60 ore) is metal in under nine seconds while shooting it apart takes most of a minute and banks nothing.

## The rig: mining upgrades, parry and dash (built)

`src/simulation/skills.rs` (`Skills`, held in the `Loadout` beside the arsenal: owned for the run, only ever raised, kept through death, cleared on restart), bought in the bench SKILLS tab with cargo. Mining upgrades (levels 0 to 4, each level 1.8x the last): beam power (+35 percent rate), beam range (+60), ore yield (+20 percent material per ore, same ore spent), magnet (+70 pickup pull) and cargo hold (+50 per material). Two abilities are locked at the start and need a fitted part of Rare or better as well as their price (level 1 unlocks, later levels improve): **parry** (plating; `parry.rs`, key `D`) and **dash** (engine; `dash.rs`, key `Shift`). Their rings on the HUD show a lock until bought, and the details panel's rig column lists them as LOCKED with the requirement.

- **Parry.** A forward arc (about 57 degrees either side, 90 reach) for 0.35 s, costing 15 shield with a 1.6 s cooldown. Each hostile shot entering it rolls once on a dedicated seeded stream: 70 percent stopped at level 1 (+6 per level, capped 95), so some of a barrage always leaks. Stopped shots are destroyed; in the first 0.12 s a stopped shot is reflected as a friendly shot at 1.5x damage and refunds 8 shield. A perfect parry (the first 0.12 s, +0.02 per level) pays out once per raise: a hit-stop of 0.06 s, a gold flash, a distinct chime, 0.6 s off the cooldown, and shield back (capped at the raise cost); reflected shots are re-aimed at the nearest creature or base in a cone about the mirror heading and seek lightly, and hit 1.5x (+0.25 per level). Re-pressing while up or cooling is refused and earns nothing.
- **Dash.** A 240 unit jump (+30 per level) toward the left stick or the ship's facing, 8 shield, 1.2 s cooldown (less per level), 0.3 s invulnerability, a swept stop short of the first rock, planetoid, wall or station (never inside one), free refusal if blocked at once, and weak latched cords snap. Trail and sound cues are presentation only. Dash consequences: creatures it passes through stagger (damped, 0.8 s unable to strike, fling or shoot); passing through a hostile shot (swallowed) or a flinger during the window is a graze, and the first graze of a dash gives +25 percent shot and burst damage (3 stacks max, 4 s, refreshed by grazes) and 5 shield back (less than the 8 cost). The details panel shows BOOST on the DASH row and orange rings orbit the ship.
- **Shoving.** `simulation/impact.rs` and the contact solver share momentum by mass ratio between the ship and any free body; the SHOVE and PLATING skills (SKILLS tab) and the beam's grip and the dash whip build on it (`simulation/shove.rs`, numbers in `tuning.rs`: `SHOVE_*`, `GRIP_*`, `WHIP_*`, `PLATING_*`; see FLOW.md "Status: shoving rocks"). The old line "nothing tethers or slings them yet" now applies to the ship; Weaver webs can tether free rocks, and ramming flings them.

## Field repair, landing pads and the bench (built)

`src/simulation/pads.rs`. Materials now have sinks beyond ammo. Keys: `E` is the one context key (gamepad B or Select; `simulation/interact.rs`): it lands on a pad in reach, builds a kit and deploys a pad over a planetoid when the hold can pay, opens and closes the bench while landed (thrust lifts off), or tithes at a seat. Field repair starts by itself after three quiet seconds (see HUD, input and feel); part insurance is always on.

- **Field repair.** Hull 5/s for 2 metal per 10 hull, then shield 6/s for 2 volatiles per 15 shield. Slow on purpose; any damage stops it and it cannot start within a second of a hit.
- **Kit and pad.** A kit costs 40 metal + 10 crystal (at most 2 carried). Deploy on a planetoid within 120 of its surface at speed below 60; one pad per planetoid, at most 6 live (the oldest is dismantled for a 50% kit refund plus its stash, spill goes to pickups). A pad has 200 hp and a stash (100 per material). Pads are keyed `(SectorId, spawn index)` in `Game::pad`, with the anchor in the planetoid's own frame, so they survive unloading and re-attach when the sector reloads (no generation change; HOME is untouched).
- **Landing.** Within 80 of a pad below speed 80; refused if a hostile rooted creature (armed rooter or alert) is within 150. The ship is rooted to the planetoid (zero velocity, turns with it). Thrust or `L` lifts off with a 140 impulse. Landed and unseen: hull +8/s, shield +20/s, and creatures notice the ship at 3x the distance (the perceived distance is tripled; hysteresis uses sight, not lose). After 4 s hidden, alert creatures lose the ship unless harmed. Firing (or any shot or mine laid), damage, or a hostile tenant on the planetoid breaks cover for 10 s; no mending happens while exposed, so a pad is not a place to tank. Mining the planetoid does not break cover. Brains get no "player landed" input (a landed ship simply has zero velocity, which they already see).
- **Bench.** Three tabs: PARTS (instant partial repair, fitted-part reforge and rarity actions, and the manual stash), WEAPONS (raise already owned profiles; unowned profiles still come from parts/charges), and SKILLS (all 19 skills grouped by mining, flight/utility, sonar, and organs, plus graft/removal). Arrows select a row/tab, Enter or Space confirms, Q takes on stash rows, E closes. There is no spare-part inventory: part actions target fitted parts. Transactions, prices, gates, repair, and stash semantics are unchanged; reforge/upgrade still draw only from the loot stream. [BENCH.md](BENCH.md) is authoritative for the reconciliation, action mapping, states, bounded layout, actual before/after purchase receipts, one-time fitted-part purchase guidance, and validation.
- **Respawn.** A death still costs a life, but with a pad the ship returns just above the nearest pad by sector distance (ties by key order), streaming that sector first. Insurance (default on) pays 10 metal before the death to keep the best part instead of dropping it. Items dropped at the wreck unload if the pad is far. A destroyed pad drops its stash as pickups and respawn falls back to the next pad or the death position. Game over and restart reset pads and cargo.
- **The counter.** Learners and civilization members that come within sight of a pad remember it (per territory for civs); those within 1500 hunt it, and within 300 they gnaw 4 hp/s each (up to 5), hurting a landed ship too. A raid from a territory that knows a pad in the simulated region arrives around the pad. Unloaded sectors simulate nothing: a pad known to enemies takes one seeded raid roll per reload (hash of seed, sector, index and reload count; 50% for 60-220 damage).

## Stations and where parts come from (built)

**There are no wild spawn points.** Ecosystem bases used to appear wherever danger, aggression, tech or swarm ran high (about 0.7 to 0.8 a sector from ring 3 on): they bred the sector's fauna, built guardians and paid a guaranteed part and two or three rolls for a few hits, which was more permanent gear per sector than every creature together and an easy upgrade farm. Generation still makes the old draws on its stream (so nothing after them moves) but places nothing; the `station` stream is retired. Wild life reproduces naturally (eggs, live birth, caps per lineage). Stone-ring nests and inhabited husks remain: they hold creatures but spawn nothing and pay no part.

Stations are now a civilization's, with the same four kinds and a turret for walls (all can be destroyed by ship weapons; hulls toughen with the square root of threat; destruction scores 500, scatters nearby creatures for ten seconds and persists across unload and reload):

- **Hive:** six organic brood chambers; 450 hull; breeds the people and harvests small rocks into guardians.
- **Foundry:** square furnace with intake arms; 650 hull; reaches farther for rocks, earns more stock and supports more guardians, with a slower brood.
- **Bastion:** armored octagon with four turret mounts; 800 hull; no brood, aimed volleys, missiles, needles, rings or spirals.
- **Depot:** radial mine magazines and ring emitter; 550 hull; seeds mines, pulses nova rings and breeds slowly.

**Drops (`tuning.rs`, "drops"; `loot.rs`).** Permanent gear is paid by what is hard to kill:

- A fallen **capital** pays `CAPITAL_PARTS` 2 parts plus one per fortress tier (0 to 3; a bastion one more) and its kind's rolls (2 or 3) plus one per tier; its first part is at least Rare from tier 1 and Epic at tier 3. An **outpost seat** pays one part and one roll (one more of each at tier 2 or more). A wall turret pays scrap only. An elder pays two parts (epic and rare) and two rolls, a third part at tier 2 or more. Capitals and elders are one-shot: a fallen civilization never returns.
- A creature's drop chance is its old chance (`0.08 + 0.4 x bounty / 400`, shared among its parts) times its hardness `0.7 + 1.0 x (hull + shield) / 100` (at most 3.5, and no more than 0.6 in all): a Bogey pays a quarter more than before, a juggernaut three and a half times.
- Apex elders are unchanged: an epic token for the slot the ship lacks, two rare parts, two rolls and all three materials.

Parts are an expected supply per sector if everything in it were killed (the `supply_tests` pin the bands): before, 2.8 at rings 3 to 5, 2.7 at 6 to 9, 2.5 at 10 to 14 and 2.3 beyond; now about 2.2, 1.7, 1.3 and 1.1, of which tough creatures give about half. Bench prices are in materials (metal, volatiles, crystal) and the materials come from mining and civilization stock, which this does not touch; wild bases added only a side trickle of about 0.16 material pickups a sector.

## Weapon patterns (built)

Stock pellets and tethers remain. Creatures and station turrets express their weapon and volley genes through the same pattern code. Missiles track the ship, burst on impact or expiry and can be shot down. Rail-style needle bursts contain 48 to 128 fast, weak particles (up to 160 through mutation); nova rings expand in every direction; spirals rotate between rapid discharges. Global projectile and mine caps bound these patterns.

Mines do no contact damage. Proximity arms a visible countdown with a blast-radius warning; leaving before detonation avoids damage. Shooting a hostile mine sets it off early. Friendly mines target enemies and spare the ship.

Ship parts and charges offer missile pods, needle cannons, mine layers and nova emitters as weapon profiles in the arsenal (owned for the run, fuelled from the cargo hold; see ROADMAP, Progression). Rotating spiral emitters and tether launchers remain enemy-only. Projectiles and creature hardpoints have distinct visual cues for their weapons.

## Tethers (built)

A cord thrower fires a cord that flies at the ship. On a hit it latches, reels in (reel speed is a gene) down to a minimum rest length (150, or its own radius plus the ship's plus 80 if larger, so a cord never holds the ship against its owner), pulls the ship with a spring, and, if its diet is siphoning, feeds on shield. A learner leads its tether shot with its brain. Losing a life cuts every latch. Bonded creatures, which the bond gene cords in pairs and clusters, hold a 300 unit cord that pulls them together if stretched; flying through it costs 14 shield and shoves the ship away. A linked cord takes three hits to cut and dies with either end.

**Cord strength is variable** (four appended tail genes, used only by tether weapons): `cord_strength` (1 to 8; pull stiffness and ceiling, 2 per unit stretch times strength, capped at 450 x (1 + strength)), `cord_slack` (200 to 3000; stretch past rest length at which the cord snaps), `cord_hardness` (1 to 10; hits to cut, 15 health per unit, a bullet does 16) and `cord_drag` (0 to 1; share of the ship's speed away from the owner bled off, at up to 4 per second). The defaults (1, 200, 2, 0) are the classic weak cord and HOME's Leech keeps them (individual variation jitters them gently, from already-drawn values so no draws move). Wild sampling (one extra final draw): about 60 percent of cord throwers stay weak, about 28 percent middling (strength 1.2 to 2.2, slack 220 to 330), about 9 percent strong (3 to 5.5, slack 500 to 1200, drag to 0.5), and about 3 percent grip (6 to 8, slack 2000 to 3000, drag 0.5 to 0.9) which the ship cannot out-thrust: shoot it, shear it or kill the owner. Danger and tech nudge the strong share up. Depth scales a cord gently: strength by 15 percent and hardness by 10 percent per point of threat above 1. Strong cords also settle the ship at the rest length instead of slinging it into the owner. Crossover passes the cord genes with the weapon; mutation drifts them for tetherers.

**Fairness for rooted owners.** A cord from a rooted creature (an immovable anchor) is capped at strength 4, hardness 4 (so at most four hits) and drag 0.5, and bleeds the ship's speed toward the anchor so it is not slung into it. Siphoning and contact damage follow the existing rules, so shooting the cord always works and a latched ship never loses shield faster than before.

**Counters.** Shooting always works. Cord shears cut a cord of 45 health or less (weak and middling) the moment it latches and wear a stouter one through at 60 health per second, so they scale with hardness.

**Readability.** Cords draw with one, two (strength 3+) or three (6+) strands, shift from violet toward hot magenta and brighten (blooming in the glow styles), tremble and swell a tension ring with load, redden near snapping, flicker when frayed and wear a clamp at the ship. A latch plays its own heavy twang (`Cue::Latch`, louder for strong cords) and the hint line says to shoot the cord while the details panel shows `TETHERED` or `GRIPPED` with a tension gauge.

**Nest builders** (`builder.rs`, generator version 25): `range::Distribution::founder` converts a salted-hash 18 percent of power-free sampled species into nest builders (calm grazers with a `builder` section, ring 4 and out). They raise structures where they live (see `simulation/build.rs`, WORKSTREAMS.md 11). The HOME golden and the start rings are untouched.

**Weaver webs** (`simulation/weave.rs`, `power::WEB_*`): the `weave` gene is live from ring 6, at its existing 1-in-330 species rate and three-ring ramp. A builder strings the nearest suitable free rock on its `power_period`, even while calm, up to `floor(2 + 4 x strength)` spokes. The warning cord is dashed and harmless for 0.9 s (1.2 s with the source farther than 760 units from the ship), with a rising plucked chord; then it is a straight mint cord, solid for 60 s. A six-spoke mint mark identifies the builder before its first web. Each cord takes three stock shots; shears wake near a web and cut it on contact. Crossing costs the existing 14 damage and 260 shove, once per 0.65 s across all contacts; grace, phase and dash prevent both. Rocks draw inward slowly (reel at most 20 units/s, pull at most 80 units/s squared), with a 140-unit clearance, spokes at least 0.55 radians apart, and no initial cord through solid bodies. At most two builders per sector and 64 cords globally; a rock cannot be shared. Nest stones, walls, planetoids and husks are excluded. Destruction, mining away an endpoint, unloading or pulling a rock past 1.25 times reach releases the web. Webs use existing bodies and add no generation draws. For a bounded render use `SSC_SPECIMEN=weaver SSC_STEPS=15` (one stationary specimen with three free rocks; see [HOOKS.md](HOOKS.md)). TODO: Spinneret organs remain unbuilt. Slinger uses separate harmless orbit cords (below).


**Slinger orbits** (`simulation/sling.rs`, `power::SLING_*`, generator version 17): from ring 6 at the existing 1-in-330 species weight, a gunless crab gathers two to four free plain, ice, or ore rocks of radius 12 to 30. Gathering runs every 0.7 s within 600 or its smaller reach, with no shared rocks or occupied hosts; two builders per sector, sixteen orbit cords globally, and the shared 64-tether budget. Harmless three-hit cords hold rotating spring targets 90 to 130 away (rock speed at most 180). An alert owner lights one settled rock and sounds a winding tone for 0.8 s (1.2 s beyond 760), showing a dotted arrow whose aim locks toward the ship at warning onset. Release applies the shared shove impulse at 600 to 800 speed; the ordinary kinetic rules determine damage, with no special multiplier. Throwing needs a ship at least 220 away, within reach, outside sanctuary, grace, and landing. Move sideways off the arrow, dash, shoot or shear a cord, mine a rock, or kill the owner. Dash cuts swept orbit cords; mining releases the beam's target. Either endpoint's death, invalidity, freezing, or unload cancels the cord and warning. Released rocks cannot be gathered for 5 s. Hostile thrown-rock kills give no ship credit; deliberate ship rams and dash whips reclaim ordinary shove behaviour. Parry still handles shots only. Tow Rig and Slinger sinew unlocks are retired or unbuilt. For bounded renders use `SSC_TELEPORT=36000,0 SSC_SPECIMEN=slinger` with `SSC_SPECIMEN_TELL=1` or `SSC_SPECIMEN_THROW=1`. ## Modular bodies (built)

A jointed creature is a tree of ordinary bodies joined by damped springs, built from the body-plan genes: spine length, taper, limb count and length, joint stiffness, wave strength, rhythm and lag. Only the head steers, with extra authority scaled by size so it can tow its body. A sine wave runs down the spine as a sideways acceleration (limbs paddle against it), which turns a straight pull into slithering. Joints are hard-limited to 1.6 times their rest length after movement and contacts, so violent impacts cannot tear a creature apart; tests cover stiffness from 30 to 900 and extreme mass and speed. Parts do not collide with their own creature. Hardpoints (every n-th part, else the head) carry the creature's weapon. Destroyed parts close ranks and a dead head promotes the next part. In easy places (threat up to about ring 3) the whole body shares one health pool instead and sheds drifting pieces as it weakens (`simulation/breakup.rs`, BESTIARY.md "Segmented bodies are compound enemies"). Not built: rigid or magnetic joint types and hardpoints derived from surface area.

## Rooted life (built)

Any species can carry a habit, five appended tail genes in `src/genome.rs`: `root` (below 0.4 free, up to 0.75 rooted while young, above that rooted for life; a body with a spine can only be rooted while young, see `Genome::habit`), `root_defense` (contact sting multiplier, and young that cling keep their weapon from 0.4), and the detach genes `detach_size` (growth share at which a young one lets go), `detach_hunger` (energy fraction below which it lets go at a poor host) and `detach_crowd` (share of the host rim above which the surplus leave). Wild sampling gives a minority of species a habit (about 7 to 26 percent, more where swarm is high and danger low, one extra final draw so earlier genes stay put); HOME species and HOME rocks are free and ordinary. Crossover passes the whole habit from the body-plan parent; mutation drifts the defense and detach genes of rooters only.

A rooted creature (`Body::root`, `simulation/root.rs`) sits at an anchor angle in its host's frame, so it rides a drifting rock and a turning planetoid, never steers, fires only into the open half-plane (never through its own rock), stings harder on contact, and can be killed, scored and looted like anything else. Hosts are free plain, ice or ore rocks and planetoids (never crystal, husk or nest stones). It feeds from the host's lichen (shared among everyone on it) plus any plankton that drifts by, and grows like any juvenile. A rooted-young habit lets go at maturity (or earlier by the detach genes) and goes free; a lifelong rooter lets go only when its host is destroyed, eaten or unloaded, when it falls free, dazed, and looks for another rock. Eggs and live young of rooting mothers are laid or born onto a rock near the mother and hatch rooted. Rooters are not prey while clinging.

Generation (`world::root_residents`, own salted stream `ROOT_SALT`, appended after everything else): every planetoid with creature budget left gets a community scaled to its radius (one per 26 units, 3 to 36), of native rooters that fit, else one to three sessile cousins of pool species (`Species::sessile`: spineless, own lineage); ordinary rocks sometimes get one or two native rooters. Rooted spawns carry `Spawn::rooted` (host spawn index, anchor, growth); a fallen host spawns its tenants free. Each counts one body against the sector budget.

## Civilizations (built)

Code: `src/territory.rs` (where and what), `src/simulation/civ.rs` (play). The learners of the previous section become the real inhabitants: rare territories held by one learner lineage, while everything else stays wildlife.

- **Where.** `world::territory(seed, sector) -> Option<Territory>` is pure. Territories live on their own lattice (`TERRITORY_CELL` 10 sectors, one per cell with chance `TERRITORY_CHANCE` 0.34, own stream `TERRITORY_SALT`): a capital sector near the cell middle and a ragged noise-edged disc of radius 2 to 3 sectors around it (a few sectors across, about 5 percent of the map). Ordinary territories keep `TERRITORY_MIN_DEPTH` (6) from HOME (the weak outpost aside, see Niches) and are biased by life and rock; the nearest sit at depth 6 to 8 (threat 2.8 to 3.4).
- **Who.** `Territory` holds an id (also the members' lineage), the capital, a strength (0.7 to 1.4) and a shape: horde, elder or both (title HORDE, COURT, DOMINION). The member species is a pool species of the capital sector turned into a learner (learner 0.75 to 1, brisk learn rate, pack, graze diet, projectile gun, 25 to 45 hull), so its name and color come from its genes. Warriors are armored escorts (their own lineage); the elder is a boss (hull 300 and twice that in play, shield 45, bounty 400, learner 1, heavy weapon, standoff and strafe, hunt diet, never breeds, never starves). All are single bodies.
- **What is generated.** Appended last on the sector's output from the territory stream (`civ_spawns`; `Spawn::civ`): the capital sector holds a base (hive or foundry for hordes, bastion or depot for courts, any for dominions) that breeds members and builds warrior guardians through the existing base code, a garrison, and the elder when the shape has one. Other member sectors follow the **density gradient** (`Territory::closeness`: 1 at the capital, a smoothstep down to 0 at `GRADIENT_PAD` 0.6 sectors past the radius). Fringe sectors (closeness under `FRINGE_BELOW` 0.3) hold one scout party of `SCOUT_MEMBERS` 2 and no stations or walls, so the approach to a civilization is a few people at the rim, then patrols and outlying posts (chance `POST_CHANCE` 0.1 to 0.5, garrison 2 to 3, patrols 2 to 3.5 members and up to two groups) toward the middle, then the capital sector itself: base, walls and turrets, the garrison and the elder. Groups lean toward the capital with closeness and outward at the rim. Members and their stations wear the civilization's tint, banner ring and the spinning learner arc, which no wildlife has. Creatures are leashed to where they were placed. Everything counts against the 220-body sector budget.
- **Behavior.** Inside their own territory members see and give up on the ship 30 percent further, and an alerted member rallies any comrade within 1600 units (only while the ship is near, so alarms cannot sustain themselves). Wildlife edges away from civil creatures. Warriors and members graze plankton; breeding, growth and learning use the usual systems (brains inherited, same-lineage crossover, lineage cap 12). Warriors and the elder are not prey; the capital base harvests rocks (existing base rules).
- **Raids.** While the ship lingers in a living territory a clock runs (`WAR_AT` 60 s, `RAID_AT` 180 s, then every `RAID_EVERY` 120 s): patrol, a war party arriving off screen and hunting the ship, then big raids (double size, the elder joins the call). Party size is `PARTY_BASE` 3 plus 2 x strength, at most `CIV_CAP` 24 alive per civilization. Leaving for `RAID_GRACE` (10 s) resets the clock. Called members answer within 2400 units.
- **Doctrine.** Every 1.5 s the trained brains of a territory are blended into a per-territory table and back (`Brain::blend_toward`), and members created later (raiders, reloaded garrisons) start with the table's weights. The table lives in the `Game`, so it survives sectors unloading; it is lost on restart (and is not saved, nothing is).
- **Falls.** Destroying the capital base or killing the elder is a lasting flag per territory (`Game::civ_fall`, beside the destroyed-spawn memory). A horde ends when its capital falls, a court when its elder dies, a dominion needs both; one half only weakens (war parties are halved, no big raid, threat verdict lower). A fallen civilization's garrison and bases do not return on reload, it sends no raids, and the territory reports FALLEN. The elder always drops two parts (one epic, one at least rare, graded 25 percent above the place through `Source::min_rarity`) and two lucky rolls, and pays a score bonus; raided elders do not exist (raids never include one).
- **Player-facing.** Entering or leaving posts a notice with the territory name, its threat and the usual SHIP POWER verdict; the HUD shows a line with name, threat, verdict, raid clock and WEAKENED; radar members and bases get a ring in the territory tint, and in-world civil creatures wear a faint banner ring. No audio beyond the existing warp-in cue on raid arrival.
- **Finding one.** The base ping always reports the nearest living civilization however far it is (`EchoKind::Nearest`, `ping.rs`): `territory::nearest_civilization` walks the 10-sector territory cells outward ring by ring (the early outpost is always a candidate, fallen civilizations are skipped, results are cached per cell in a `SeatCache`) and stops when no farther ring can beat the best, up to `SEARCH_CELLS` 24 cells (240 sectors, far past any real gap). The answer is the nearest sector of that territory (its capital's, if the ship is already inside). It sounds when the ring reaches it, or as the ring dies if it is beyond the ping's range, and draws as a crosshair blip when on screen and a double-chevron edge arrow in the civilization's tint otherwise, always the first echo arrow; the HUD adds a short line under the region name (`<name> <n> SECTORS <compass>`) with a small arrow toward it. The other echo kinds keep their range. From HOME it finds the early outpost.
- **Tuning knobs.** `GRADIENT_PAD`, `FRINGE_BELOW`, `CORE_ABOVE`, `SCOUT_MEMBERS`, `PATROL_MEMBERS`, `POST_MEMBERS`, `POST_CHANCE`, `TERRITORY_CHANCE`, `TERRITORY_CELL`, `TERRITORY_MIN_DEPTH`, shape weights and radius range in `territory.rs`; garrison sizes (`share`) and genome clamps in `Territory::member`, `warrior`, `elder`; `Territory::menace` (HUD threat multiplier); `WAR_AT`, `RAID_AT`, `RAID_EVERY`, `RAID_GRACE`, `PARTY_BASE`, `CIV_CAP`, `DOMAIN_SIGHT`, `COORD_RANGE`, `CALL_RANGE`, `SHOO_RANGE`, `TABLE_PULL`, `MEMBER_PULL`, `ELDER_HULL`, `ELDER_SCORE` in `civ.rs`.
- **Known gaps.** Balance is by numbers, not playtested: a bare ship is outclassed in the nearest territories (depth 6 to 8). TODO: doctrine and falls are not persisted past the session. Wildlife is edged away unless it has an affinity for the civilization (see Species and civilizations). TODO: no border cue in the world and no dedicated sounds.

## Diplomacy (built)

Code: `src/simulation/diplomacy.rs`, numbers in `tuning.rs` (`REGARD_*`, `HURT_*`, `KILL_*`, `MINE_*`, `TITHE_*`, `TRADE_*`, `DOCTRINE_PULL`). Each civilization met has a regard toward the ship (-100 to 100, kept for the run, lost on restart) read as a tier with hysteresis: hostile, wary, ignores, friendly. Ordinary civilizations start at 0 (ignores), the early outpost at 15. It falls with damage to members (0.08 a point), structures (0.02), kills by the ship (member 5, warrior 7, elder 30, turret 8, outpost seat 30, capital 45) and ore taken in a claim (0.12 each); it rises 0.1 a second while the ship lingers in the claim 20 s after the last offence (to 25, the outpost to 60), fades back toward its start away from the claim, and jumps by a tithe (+9). Non-hostile civilizations: members do not attack on sight or join alarms (a hurt member still fights), no raids, turrets and bastions hold fire, nobody hunts a pad, doctrine tables learn slower (`DOCTRINE_PULL`). Friendly: charts shared once, the doctrine table forgotten, a tithe returns a repair or a swap. Wildlife has ties to civilizations (see Species and civilizations), so killing it can cost or earn regard.

## Species and civilizations (built)

Code: `src/affinity.rs` (pure), `src/simulation/wildlife.rs` (play), numbers in `src/affinity.rs` and `tuning.rs` (`FAUNA_*`, `MAX_*`, `*_KILL_*`, `GAIN_*`, `TAG_*`). Wildlife is not uniformly hostile or passive toward a civilization: every (species lineage, civilization) pair has a deterministic affinity from -1 (hostile) to 1 (friendly), at or beyond 0.3 either way a disposition.

- **Affinity.** A hash of the lineage and civilization id, a bias read from the genome (hunters, rock eaters, tether throwers, flingers, negative mass and rage lean hostile; harm-triggered, schooling and grazing species lean friendly), a regional noise term from `range::regional_noise` sampled where the wildlife is (so one species can be hostile to a civilization in one corner of its range and friendly in another), a base shift, and a warmer shift for the peaceful early outpost. It never reads the species' attitude to the ship: a species can be passive to the player yet hostile to a civilization, or the reverse. About a third of real pairings are hostile, a third neutral and a quarter to a third friendly.
- **Hostile.** Within `HOSTILE_REACH` of a civil body it hates, wildlife sets upon it and bites (`FAUNA_BITE` plus half its contact damage, times threat). At most `MAX_ATTACKERS` per body and `MAX_ASSAULT` per civilization (`MAX_ASSAULT_PEACEFUL` for a settlement) at once, and a settlement takes `FAUNA_PEACEFUL_SCALE` of the damage. Structures take `FAUNA_STRUCTURE_SCALE` and neither they nor an elder can be taken below `FAUNA_STRUCTURE_FLOOR` of their hull by wildlife (a fall is the ship's doing). Idle people hunt hostile wildlife near their post and strike at range; turrets fire on it. Kills either way are eaten-style: no score, no loot.
- **Friendly.** Not shooed away, not attacked, not drawn into alarms; it drifts toward the settlement and holds at `HERD_RING`, forming mixed herds beside its people. Neutral wildlife is left as before (it edges away from civil creatures).
- **Consequences.** Killing friendly wildlife near a civilization's people costs regard (`FRIEND_KILL_COST` times affinity, banner "they valued this herd"). Killing hostile wildlife earns a little (`HOSTILE_KILL_GAIN` times how hated, shrinking by `GAIN_DIMINISH` per kill, at most `GAIN_CAP` per `GAIN_WINDOW`; banner "they thank you"), so it cannot be farmed. The HUD lists up to three nearby hostile or friendly species under the territory line, the star map's detail block reads a charted sector's wildlife toward the nearby civilization, and the sector map has a "wildlife vs civ" layer (friendly, hostile, mixed) and a hover line for sectors in or beside a territory.
- **Known gaps.** Fights are instant strikes and bites, not projectiles. Wildlife targets only the nearest civil body. Affinity is not persisted anywhere (it is pure). Wildlife-versus-wildlife is unchanged.

## Apex elders (built)

Code: `src/apex.rs` (generation, archetypes, names, strength), `src/simulation/apexes.rs` (play), numbers `APEX_*` in `tuning.rs` and the consts at the top of both files. A rare named boss roams a sector: a member of one of the sector's species grown very old (`Genome::individual_from` at the wide end), stamped with an **archetype** and given an **animal body** (generator version 20, `apex::body`): the plan seed and archetype come from the source species lineage (`AnimalSpecimen::for_entity` on a `Domain::Animal` stream, then `misshapen()`), so a species keeps one silhouette whatever archetype its elder is stamped with; the genome `radius` and `hull` are scaled by `ELDER_SCALE` (1.6) and the body is cut back (depth, then segments, then limb length) to reach at most `MAX_REACH` (14) head radii. The head is the whole fight: friendly shots, blasts and mines pass through the trailing parts (plain armour, no weak points yet, `TODO:` weak points per node kind: eye, organ, actuator), the bubble, guard and resistances stay on the head, the body falls with the head in the same step and only the head pays the kill, bounty and hoard (`apex_of` is the head only, `is_apex_part` the rest). A power that needs a single body (a phantom blink, mimic) or a sector too full for the body keeps the old single body, so which sectors hold an apex never changes. An elder hurt from beyond its sight is alert (so the sniper counters fire). Chance per sector is 2 percent from ring 5 and 0.4 percent for a half-strength lesser one at rings 3 and 4; it is the last spawn of its sector on its own salted stream, so no index moves and HOME is untouched.

- **Archetypes** (`apex::archetype`, a pure pick from the sector hash weighted by its biome, so predator country breeds hunters and juggernauts, plains queens, strange lands maelstroms and phantoms): *juggernaut* (huge hull, a five-shot fan, a telegraphed 0.9 s windup then a 1.1 s charge at 800 that rams), *swarm queen* (nova rings and a retinue of up to 4 escorts, 7 enraged, raised every 5.5 s within the usual body and sector caps), *lasher* (tether weapon with strong long cords, siphon diet: cord strength 5.5, slack 1800, hardness 7, drag 0.6), *phantom* (small and fast, needle bursts, blinks to 340 to 520 units from the ship every 3.4 s), *bulwark* (plated front arc: friendly shots within about 75 degrees of its facing do 12 percent damage), *pack hunter* (a perfect lead gene, no brain, missiles, pack social so it alarms neighbours), *maelstrom* (negative mass, fling 2, nova rings, and every 8 s a 1.3 s pull at 850 that drags the ship in) and *warden* (a four-arm rotating spiral). Weapons, cords, lead and mass are genes; the charge, escorts, blink, pull and plates are in `apexes.rs` keyed by the archetype (not by species). Each has its own radius, sides, aspect, tint, epithets and crown (spoke count).
- **Strength.** Hull and shield are not genes (genes are bounded) but `apex::hull` and `apex::shield`, applied when the body is made: base hull 2400 (phantom) to 5000 (juggernaut) plus 160 shield, growing 2 percent per ring past 5 (capped at 3x), halved for a lesser one. The depth threat still divides the damage taken and sharpens what it deals, so a ring 5 juggernaut absorbs about 15000 against a bare ship's 160 damage per second (the old elder had at most 400 hull). A prepared ship should find them hard and possible.
- **Phase change.** Below 35 percent hull every apex enrages once (`APEX: NAME enrages`): 25 percent faster, fire period 0.65x, contact sting 1.2x, a bulwark sheds its plates, signature moves come faster and the crown reddens.
- **Play.** When one comes within 3600 units the ship posts `APEX: NAME stirs`; the HUD shows a gold crown and hull bar with the name and distance at the top (the details panel adds archetype, ENRAGED and HUNTING YOU), the radar a gold ring, the world a golden crown, and an always-on gold double-chevron edge arrow follows the nearest within 9000. Death pays score, 90 of each material, three parts (an epic in the slot of a still-locked ability: plating for parry, then engine for dash, else cannon; two rare) and two lucky rolls, is recorded by name in the run summary (`APEX SLAIN: ...`) and is not an extirpation. It is remembered by spawn index like any kill, so it never returns. The sector map's hover tip names the archetype.

## Civilization mining and fortified cities (built)

Civilizations also build small structures (`simulation/build.rs`, workstream 11 slice 3): a member who is not mining lays a few dozen tinted blocks near where it stands, three per territory per session, in a style fixed by the territory id. See WORKSTREAMS.md section 11.

Code: `src/simulation/civmine.rs` (mining), `src/fortress.rs` (pure layouts), `src/simulation/fortress.rs` (walls and turrets in play), `Territory::{fort_tier, fort_archetype, turret_arms, fortification}` in `src/territory.rs`.

- **Mining.** Rank-and-file members (`CivRole::Member`, the "workers") of a territory mine rocks inside it by the player's lode rules (`Game::drain_rock`: shrink toward the 14 floor, crumble and record as fallen, spent ore in the shared `mined` map, so a civilization can drain a planetoid before the player arrives). At most `MAX_MINERS` (2) per territory, one rock each, never shared. A miner is the lowest-spawn-index free generated member and its rock one of the three lowest-index minable rocks within `SEARCH` (2600) with a clear line (no wall), picked with `civ_rng`; crystal, husks and the rock under the ship's beam are skipped. A miner with the ship within `FLEE_RANGE` (900) and no warrior or elder within 520 runs and rests; with an escort it keeps working. A miner stuck for `PATIENCE` (25 s) gives up. Ore worked is capped per territory per session (`ORE_BUDGET` 450 + 200 x strength), so a civilization cannot strip a sector.
- **The stash.** `civ_mining[territory].stock` (metal, volatiles, crystal; total cap `STOCK_CAP` 150) feeds the capital base: while mining is active the passive trickle is off and the base draws `FEED_RATE` (1.4/s) from the stash, only while it has a guardian slot, so surplus piles up. A stocked capital shows a cache marker (crates in the colors of the materials, a tint ring) and working miners show a beam in the territory tint. If the capital falls, half of each material drops as pickups (`SPILL`) and the rest is lost.
- **Fortresses.** Capitals get a full fortress and some outposts (chance 0.45 + 0.12 x tier) a lighter one, appended last on the sector's output on their own stream (`FORT_SALT`); indices and HOME are untouched. Walls are pinned `RockKind::Wall` bodies (overlapping circles, radius 38, spacing 66): solid for ships and creatures, stop every shot (piercing too), tough (`WALL_HULL` x depth), never mined, no shards, no loot, no score. Enemy fire and blasts never wear a wall down; the ship's shots, mines and rams do, and a destroyed piece is persisted by spawn index (`fallen`). A fallen civilization's walls remain as ruins; its turrets are not regenerated and go quiet.
- **Archetypes** (chosen from the territory id; hordes build grids or rings, courts stars or spirals, dominions any): **ring** (concentric rings with offset gates, so the way in winds through the annulus), **spiral** (one coiling wall, the corridor between its turns is the road), **star** (bastion star, turrets on the tips, gates in the faces), **grid** (city blocks: a random spanning tree over cells plus extra doors around an open plaza). Each draws differently in the territory tint: masonry octagons with a parapet, round coil pods, long diamonds, square slabs.
- **Scale.** `Territory::fort_tier` 0 to 3 from capital depth and strength (the nearest territories are tier 0 to 2). Body budgets per capital tier 72, 92, 112, 130 and 34 per outpost (`CAPITAL_BUDGET`, `OUTPOST_BUDGET`); a layout over budget degrades to a lower tier, then a plain ring, and is dropped if obstacles (planetoids, nest stones, wells, other stations) leave no way in. On load, pieces are skipped once the world is within `RESERVE` (220) bodies of `MAX_BODIES`.
- **Connectivity.** Every layout is flood-filled on a 14-unit grid with the ship's radius plus margin as clearance before it is returned; there is always a way from outside to within 230 of the heart, or the layout is degraded or dropped. Gates are about 125 units wide.
- **Turrets.** `BaseKind::Turret` bases mounted on wall pieces at corners, tips and gate flanks, with a fire arc and a barrel that tracks the ship. They fire the pattern of the territory's own genes (`Territory::turret_arms`: the weapon of the capital's pool species, else the first armed relative, mapped to projectile, missile, needles, nova or spiral) within 950 units and only with a clear line: walls are cover, and creatures also hold fire without a line to the ship. Gate turrets keep one or two hostile mines laid at their chokepoint. A turret has 170 hull, pays scrap metal and sometimes a lucky roll, no part, no score beyond 60 x threat, and does not count as a station kill.
- **Defenders.** Warriors (and every third member) of a fortified capital stand at the gates; they are the same learners, so the shared doctrine applies unchanged.
- **HUD.** The details panel's territory line shows `ARCHETYPE FORT n`; `Territory::menace` rises by a tenth per tier step, so the threat verdict counts fortification.
- **Tuning knobs.** `SEG_RADIUS`, `SEG_SPACING`, `GATE_HALF`, `CAPITAL_BUDGET`, `OUTPOST_BUDGET`, per-archetype tables in `fortress.rs`; `fort_tier` and `menace` in `territory.rs`; `WALL_HULL`, turret `hull` (`BaseKind::Turret`), `TURRET_REACH`, `PERIOD`, `LAY_*` in `simulation/fortress.rs`; `MAX_MINERS`, `ORE_BUDGET`, `YIELD`, `FEED_RATE`, `FLEE_RANGE`, `SPILL`, `STOCK_CAP` in `civmine.rs`.
- **Known gaps.** Miners are found only among generated members with a clear line, so fort garrisons rarely mine (outposts and patrols do). Fortress balance is by numbers, not playtested; wall and turret damage is not persisted (only destruction). Raiders and members do not path through mazes (they steer straight and bump walls). No dedicated sounds.

## Sector map (built)

`src/sectormap.rs` and the thin binary `src/bin/sectormap.rs` sample the same pure functions the game uses (`world::generate`, the niche ecology fields, rings, regions, territories with the early outpost, planetoids with `renewable`, apex spawns, HOME) over a grid and write ONE self-contained HTML file (inline SVG, CSS and JS, no network). It needs no Bevy app or GPU and builds with `--no-default-features`.

```sh
cargo run --no-default-features --bin sectormap -- --seed 5460803 --cols 200 --rows 200 --out map.html
```

Options: `--seed` (decimal or `0x` hex; default the game's `0x535343`), `--cols` and `--rows` (1 to 256, default 41), `--center X,Y` (default 0,0), `--out` (default `sectormap.html`). The same options give byte-identical output. The page shows the lead species' hue (or, with the species dropdown or a click on a species in a pinned sector, only where that one lives), life tint, rock shade (hatched belts), layers for per-sector diversity (the species count), biome cells and kinds, and rock belts with oases, a summary strip (species per sector histogram, species spread counts, mean life, belt share, oases, biome shares), gold borders on confluences, gold borders on confluences, Moore rings from HOME, region and territory outlines, icons for HOME, planetoids (green ring when renewable), gravity wells (drawn by mode, with orbits and hop reach), a powers layer (creatures carrying a rare power, and the carrier species named in the hover tip), capitals (fort tier pips), outposts, the early outpost and apexes; layer toggles, a legend, hover tips, pan and zoom, click-to-pin with a detail panel and a coordinate jump box. Bump `GENERATOR_VERSION` when the page or its data layout changes. Not a game rule: nothing in the simulation reads it.

## Rare powers and dynamic wells (built)

Design in [BESTIARY.md](BESTIARY.md); this records what is built.

- **The gene block** (`src/power.rs`, tail of `genome!`): `power_period`, `power_reach`, `power_hold` and twenty-two intensity genes (`phase`, `repel`, `warp`, `lens`, `blink`, `bypass`, `emp`, `glare`, `mimic`, `latch`, `symbiote`, `cloud`, `devour`, `weave`, `song`, `dim`, `rift`, `sling`, `rune`, `split`, `confuse` and `engulf`, the last one appended), all dormant at zero. The gate is 0.3 (`power::GATE`); effect strength is `(|v| - 0.3) / 0.7`. `Genome::sample` takes one more final draw and gives a species at most one power (about 7 percent of far species); an individual of any species awakens a Mild or Strange one at 1 in 400 from ring 3 (`Genome::individual_in`, no extra draw); crossover passes the block whole from the body-plan parent; `mutate` nudges only carried intensities and never below 0.32; `drifted` skips the block and `wild_min_ring` raises a carrier's floor to its power's first ring; civilization members are cleared. HOME and the golden test do not move. Only built powers (`Power::built`) are ever sampled or awakened, so a carrier never does nothing; the built set is blink, phase, bypass, emp, glare, dim, confuse, repel, warp, lens, devour, split, cloud, song, mimic, latch, symbiote, weave, sling, rune, rift and engulf (roughly 6 to 7 percent of far species carry one). All current creature powers are built; their player organ ideas have separate status in BESTIARY.md.
- **Blink** (`simulation/powers.rs`): an alert single-bodied carrier more than 220 from the ship picks a landing point on a ring around it, announces it for 0.35 s (a ring at the spot, a chime), hops, and fires a snap shot within 0.2 s. The apex Phantom is a creature with `blink` 1, period 3.4 and reach 520 (`apex.rs`); the archetype branch in `apexes.rs` is gone.
- **Phase**: a pure cycle (`Genome::phase_at`) of solid and phased windows; a phased body (`Body::phased`) is skipped by friendly shots, homing, blasts, mines, the contact solver, its own gun and contact damage, and shows dim with an afterimage; the last 0.4 s brighten with a chime. The solid window is never under 1 s.
- **Bypass**: `Bullet::pith` is the share (0.2 + 0.6 strength, at most 0.8) of a hostile shot's damage that skips the shield; the carrier's shots are capped at 260 speed and drawn violet, with a charge ring 0.35 s before each shot and a dull tick on hit.
- **Jam status** (`simulation/jam.rs`): one status on the ship shared by every disabler. A jam takes one or two of weapons, dash, parry and boost (never dash with parry, never all; the HUD besides when rolled) for at most 1.5 s, and is refused while another is running, within 6 s of the last one ending, while landed or in respawn grace. Weapons fire, `dash`, `parry` and the boosts check `jammed`; a refused press cues a flat buzz. The HUD greys the ring with static and the seconds left; a jammed HUD shows only static. **Emp** (Stormcap): an alert carrier within its ring (power_reach clamped to 200..420, on screen) charges a ring that closes over 0.9 s (never under 0.6, one charge in the world at a time, cancelled when its shield breaks or, shieldless, when it is hit); a ship inside when it closes is jammed for 0.8 + 0.7 strength seconds. **Confuse** (Dizzard, new gene `confuse`): same ring and rules, but it bends the pilot's input: aim and move are rotated by a swaying angle of 0.3 + 0.4 strength radians (`scramble_input`, a pure function of the status), the turn is inverted for the first 0.3 s only from strength 0.7. The HUD shows a pink halo, a reticle that sways with the offset and a tick where the ship truly aims. **Glare** (Argus Moth): eyes open for 0.7 s, then a presentation-only screen glitch of 1 to 2 s (scanlines, torn rows, red and cyan fringes, false radar blips, shuffled edge arrows), never during grace, one at a time and 3 s apart; nothing in the rules reads it and bullets and hazards are never touched. Under reduce effects (`U`) the screen overlay is off and the static stops flickering. **Dim** (Gloomfeeder): a field of power_reach * 1.2 (`Game::dim_at`) darkens the nebula and stars to at most 35 percent of normal, stops plankton taking root inside, fades creatures to 55 percent (never their outline), and a creature notices a ship that is not firing at 70 percent of its range; bullets are never dimmed. Elder stamps (major apexes from ring 7): Maelstrom emp, Warden glare, Phantom confuse beside its blink. `SSC_JAM=emp|confuse|glitch|hud` jams the ship for a screenshot.
- **Gravity powers** (`simulation/fields.rs`): every force on the ship is capped at 60 percent of its thrust, none does damage. **Repel** (Pushwhale): outward field `420 s (1 - d/reach)^2`, shots bend away; each period it inhales for 1.0 s (the field reverses to 35 percent) and then a ring shoves everything inside by `600 s`; drawn as drifting field rings, a closing pale ring and the shove ring. **Warp** (Tarbloom): a bubble of radius power_reach, one acting per sector (lowest id); slow runs bodies and shots at `1 - 0.45 s` (never under 0.55; the ship's top speed is floored the same), shots regain their speed on leaving; haste runs hostile shots and creatures' guns at `1 + 0.4 s`; drawn as a shimmering rim (blue slow, orange haste). **Lens** (Lenswyrm): a pocket well of 0.4 s of a full one out to 0.8 power_reach and a bend on shots; its radar blip is drawn up to 120 units off (display only). **Devour** (Tidegorger): eats free rocks it touches, growing 3 percent per rock (radius, mass and hull together) up to `1 + 2 s`; from gene 0.6 it eats a weak static well it touches (8 percent per second), holds up to a full pocket well, and if it dies holding 0.3 or more the well is released where it fell and fades over 90 s.
- **Body powers**: **Split** (`simulation/split.rs`, Splitter): on death, two pieces (three from gene 0.7) appear 0.4 s later with a third of the hull, 0.7 of the radius, 1.15 of the speed and 0.3 of the bounty, never splitting again; the queue is capped at 12 and obeys the body budgets; the seam brightens as the body nears death. **Cloud** (Murmur): one body whose shots entering the radius are swallowed with chance 0.25 + 0.35 s (once per shot, from a hash), only the core (0.3 of the radius) takes shots, blasts hurt fully, the ship inside is stung 10 + 6 s per second, and bodies pass through it; drawn as motes. **Song** (`simulation/song.rs`, Dirgewhale): a dirge opens its mouth 0.7 s, then sends a ring at 500 per second out to power_reach with an 80-unit safe gap placed 0.7 to 1.4 radians off the line to the ship; outside the gap it hurts 25, shoves 250 and, from strength 0.7, jams the weapons 0.6 s (under the jam rules); at most 6 rings fly; a negative song is a chant that makes creatures within reach fire 25 percent faster. **Mimic** (`simulation/mimic.rs`, Lurefish): poses as a rock (below gene 0.6, drifting toward the ship at up to 38) or a pickup on a stalk (hanging still) while quiet and unarmed, left off arrows, radar and the pressure count; it cracks for 0.3 s and then hunts when the ship is within half its reach, when it is hurt, or after the ship idles inside its reach for 2 s. `SSC_SPECIMEN=skipjack|veilwing|hullpick|stormcap|argus|gloomfeeder|dizzard|pushwhale|tarbloom|lenswyrm|tidegorger|splitter|murmur|dirgewhale|lurefish|hullworm|remora|weaver|slinger|runekeeper|seamer` places specimens (one staged builder for Weaver, Slinger, Runekeeper, or Seamer, three creatures otherwise).

## Realms (built)

A very large granularity layer over the map (`src/realm.rs`), built from the same machinery as biomes (Voronoi cells with additive weights and a noise-warped edge) at a much larger scale: an 80 sector lattice with weights up to 22 and a 5 sector warp, so a realm runs about 40 to 120 sectors across (a test pins the median side). A biome is a country; a realm is a continent, and where a biome says who lives there, a realm says what kind of strong the place tests. The aim is in `docs/FLOW.md`, "Balance dimensions": a big gun that blasts everything in one realm should find itself puny in the next.

- **A pure function** of the master seed and a sector (`realm::realm`; `realm::weighting` is the same without spelling the name, for the generation paths). HOME is the **Cradle**, a realm of its own that holds at least ring 14 outright and in practice a region of hundreds of sectors; it has no lottery weight, applies nothing, and a golden-pinned start is untouched (every effect is exactly neutral there, so no earlier draw moves). Past it each lattice cell rolls one of the catalog's other kinds by `Spec::roll`.
- **The catalog** is data (`realm::CATALOG`, a table of `Spec` rows): title, a name word (Aelion *Shroud*, Kordthur *Bastion*; soft or harsh phonemes from `region`), a colour, the axes it tests (one or two primary, one mild), the axes it favours, a blurb, its `Effects`, the biomes and elder archetypes it weights, the powers its elders carry and whether its elders are bubbled. Ten rows: the Cradle, **The Veil** (dust: weapon range -35%, sensors -45%, enemies 20% quicker; Phantoms and Hunters; Blink elders), **Dead Reach** (jam carriers x4, jams 40% longer, dash and parry fizzle a quarter of the time, mining +25%; Maelstrom and Warden elders with EMP, glare and confusion), **The Crush** (gravity +70%, wells +120%; Lens elders), **Hive Marches** (swarms +140%, creatures +40%; queens and lashers; Split elders), **Iron Tide** (enemy shield +140%, hull +40%, 6 points of flat plating off every hit, every elder bubbled, mining +30%; bulwarks and juggernauts; Bypass elders), **Glass Seas** (enemy hull -55%, shield -60%, 35% quicker, swarms +80%, creatures +70%; Phase elders), **Quiet Gold** (the rest realm: threat -40%, elders -50%, creatures -20%, mining +60%), **Hungry Deep** (hunters +220%, enemies quicker and harder) and **Bright Silence** (creatures -82%, elders +350%).
- **One view-model.** Everything reads a `Realm` and nothing decides what a realm does elsewhere: species niche weights and biome affinity (`species_weight`, `biome_weight` in `range::weights`: hunters and packs, schoolers and broods, jam carriers; the rock belt's mask is applied after, so a belt is still barren), elder archetype weights and chance (`apex::archetype`, `apex::rank`), enemy hull, shield, pace, damage and plating (`Phenotype::foe`, applied in `damage_bypassing` and creature steering, so growth cannot undo it), creature counts and wells in `world::compose_with`, the realm's power stamps on elders (`apex::stamp_realm`, 70 percent of majors, a Phantom keeps its blink), the ship's weapon reach (`control_player`, the missile and nova launchers), sensors (ping range, mineral and threat arrows), gravity (`apply_gravity`), mining yield, jam time and the dash and parry fizzle (`simulation/realms.rs`: a deterministic hash of the seed, the time and the ability, so replays agree; a fizzle costs nothing and locks the key 0.6 s). `threat` scales how far depth threat rises above one.
- **A slope, never a step.** `Realm::intensity` rises from nothing at a border to one ten sectors in (`EDGE_RAMP`) and from nothing at ring 16 to one at ring 28 (`FAR_FROM`, `FAR_RAMP`); every effect is a lerp from neutral, held inside `MIN_MULT` to `MAX_MULT` (and `MAX_PLATING`), so tests pin monotonic and bounded.
- **Announcing it.** `simulation/realms.rs` tracks the realm with the same hysteresis as regions (hold 4 s, at least 30 s between banners, `tuning::REALM_*`): `ENTERING THE REALM OF <NAME>` as an Epic notice, never for the starting Cradle, and the realms visited are counted in the run summary (`REALMS` and a `REALMS VISITED` line). The HUD has a realm tag under the threat pips (name, kind, a tinted diamond with the glyph of the first axis it tests and a dot per further one, a calm ring where it tests nothing); the details panel gives the name, the kind, what it tests and what it changes, biggest first; the star map's detail line names the realm of a charted sector.
- **Colour.** Each kind has a tint (and a small per-realm shift); the nebula takes it on in proportion to the realm's strength (`backdrop::REALM_TINT`), and the sector map has a realm layer (`generator version 17`): a tint per realm, a heavy line at its border, its name, a legend row and, in the hover tip and pinned panel, the realm, its kind and strength. Rows end with the realm index and strength.

## Sniping counters (built)

The other half of the answer to "big guns blast everything from a safe distance". Numbers are in `simulation/tuning.rs` ("sniping counters") and `arsenal.rs`.

- **Adaptive resistance** (`simulation/adapt.rs`). A tough creature (hull plus shield of at least 420) or any apex builds resistance to the damage family (`arsenal::Family`: Kinetic for the stock gun, spread, flanks and homing; Needle; Lance; Explosive for missiles, mines, nova and blasts) that has hurt it. Each hit adds `3 x dealt / pool` to its family's meter (a third of the pool dealt by one family fills it); resistance is `0.55 x meter` of the damage, so a hit always lands for at least 45 percent. A meter bleeds 0.02 a second, and 0.09 more once its family has not hit for 1.5 s, so switching guns (`[` and `]`) relieves it in about ten seconds. The apex bar shows a pip per family (bigger and brighter the more hardened) and the details panel says `HARDENED  KINETIC -40%  (switch guns with [ ])`; a creature shows its pips above its hull.
- **Weapon tradeoffs.** Each profile has a sweet spot and a mild falloff (`Profile::reach`, e.g. the stock gun full to 800 then down to 60 percent over 1400 more, the lance full to 1100 then 55 percent, the shotgun spread 450 and 45 percent; never zero). A shot carries its origin and its profile, so the falloff is measured at the hit. Heavy shots (a damage stat above the stock gun's) are slower (`1 / (1 + 0.06 per stock-gun of excess)`, at least 60 percent) so they can be led and dodged, and each trigger pull kicks the ship by `0.2 x (damage over stock) x profile weight`, at most 28 (the lance 1.6, the stock gun 1, a needle burst 0.4, launchers none). Fuel profiles already cost cargo.
- **Elders that close the distance** (`simulation/apexes.rs`, `closers`). An elder hurt from beyond 1000 for 5 seconds in all lunges (a planted 0.7 s telegraph, then a second at 720 along the ship) unless it has a closer of its own (a juggernaut charges, a phantom blinks, a lasher reels, a maelstrom pulls). Realm stamps add blinks, lenses and jams to more elders.
- **Barrages.** An alert elder with the ship beyond 1100 plants, rings the ground and the line to where the ship will be (1.2 s), then sends a fan of 9 (13 enraged) slow shots (360 fast, 1.2 of a pellet each, reach 1800, spaced 0.11 rad so there are gaps at any range) every 8 seconds (5 enraged).
- **Bubbles.** A Warden's, and every elder's in Iron Tide: shots from beyond 520 of the elder leak 15 percent of their damage; a lance shot passes whole; a shot from inside the range goes through and wears the bubble (10 percent of the elder's pool in close damage breaks it for 9 s, `BUBBLE BROKEN`, after which it re-forms whole; an unhurt one mends). It draws as a pale ring that thins and then breaks into a dashed one, with a thin bar on the apex HUD.
- **Pinned by tests** (`apexes.rs`, `adapt.rs`, `arsenal.rs`): accrual and decay, the cap, switching relieves it (as numbers and in play), falloff bands, recoil and slow bounds, a scripted duel (a ship with six times the damage and a long gun parked 3000 from a pack hunter and from a swarm queen for 90 seconds: either the elder survives, or it takes at least 30 s and the ship loses at least 600 hull and shield; it hardens to 40 percent or more kinetic and uses a lunge or a barrage; two runs agree), a barrage's telegraph, speed and gaps, a lunge, the bubble's rules and the realm stamps.

## Sky: the backdrop (built)

The sky behind the ship says where you are, in the colours of the sector map. `src/backdrop.rs` is a pure, headless function of the seed and a world position (`backdrop_at`): each sector contributes a look read from the same ecology the map samples (the lead species' hue shaded by life, the biome hue as the second colour, rock brown, belt depth, oasis, confluence, territory colour, apex), the four sector centres around the point are blended bilinearly (eased, so a place keeps its own look until a border nears), and so every value is continuous across borders. Looks are cached per sector, since `ecology` and `region` are not cheap.

- Places differ in grain, not just hue: plains wide soft wisps, predator and rough country dark streaks, strange and keen lands filaments, belts dust and grit with few stars (and a brown cast), reaches (gaps) cold, dark and sparse, oases a warm glow pocket around the planetoid, confluences two hues with a bright core and many stars, territories a faint haze in the civilization's colour with a stronger edge at the claim boundary, apex sectors a dark and gold vignette. Near a region border a seam rises toward it, tinted by the regions that meet there. Star density and colour follow the region.
- `src/nebula.rs` only draws: seven low-alpha meshes behind everything (far and mid wisps, streaks, filaments, grit, a smooth colour wash, the vignette), each tiling one noise texture the library generates once (`backdrop::texture`), tinted per vertex from a coarse grid of `backdrop_at` samples, on parallax layers. No layer is ever more opaque than `MAX_LAYER_ALPHA`, so ships, rocks and shots stay readable.
- `U` toggles "reduce effects" (hides the nebula and returns the stars to the plain field); `SSC_REDUCE_EFFECTS=1` starts that way. `SSC_OFFSCREEN=1` renders into an image instead of the window, which makes `SSC_SCREENSHOT` work when the display is locked or asleep (a window renders black then).

## Open question

The planning notes describe sectors of 1200 by 1200 units. They are 6000 by 6000 today (`world::SECTOR_SIZE`), several screens across. The generation policy scales counts by parameter rather than by area, so changing the size means retuning densities. Which scale feels right is worth deciding by playing.

## Run stats and extirpation (built)

Code: `src/simulation/run.rs`; counters bumped at the existing event sites (`remove_destroyed`, `move_bullets`, mining, eggs, pads, loot, civ falls). Player-facing text says "sector" for what the code calls a sector.

- **Run stats** (`Game::run`, `RunStats`): kills (total, by lineage, elders, stations, civilizations toppled, juveniles, eggs, creatures lost to predators or hunger), sectors explored (distinct sectors the ship has been in), deepest depth and threat faced, ore mined by material, rocks worked out, planetoids drained, shots, damage dealt and taken, deaths, weapons unlocked, pads built, parts found and distance flown. Time and score are `Game::time` and `Game::score`. `Game::reset` rebuilds everything, so a run is launch to game over. A summary panel (`Game::run_report`) shows at game over, and a short recap for a few seconds after each lost ship. The session best score is kept in memory only (`Session`), no save file.
- **Epithet.** The summary and the recap carry a silly title from `simulation/titles.rs`, a pure function of the run's facts (`TitleFacts`) with a fixed priority: Apex Hunter; Scourge of the REGION (2 extirpations, the region of longest stay); Genocidal Prospector (an extirpation and 150 ore); Friend of the CIVILIZATION; Tithe Payer (5 tithes); Parry Dancer (10 perfect parries); Blink Addict (40 dashes); Gentle Cartographer (12 sectors, 3 regions, at most one kill per 3 sectors); Reckless Wreck (three ships lost in under 6 minutes); Pacifist Prospector (300 ore, 10 kills at most); else Wandering Hazard. Thresholds are `TITLE_*` in `tuning.rs`; ties go to the longer stay or warmer regard, then the lower key.
- **Species extirpation.** A species' range is the group of sectors, joined by the 8-neighborhood (a shared corner joins them), where its lineage is generated (a creature spawn, or a husk's tenants). It is found by flood fill from the sector of a kill, bounded to `RANGE_RADIUS` (8) sectors from there, cached per lineage, and a pure function of the seed. A lineage is extirpated from a range when every generated spawn of it in the range is in `fallen` (spawns of a fallen civilization count as gone) and no live creature body or egg of it sits in a range sector. Checked only when a creature or egg of the lineage is lost (killed, eaten, starved, broken or spoiled), never per tick. Each (lineage, range) counts once: "Species extirpated: NAME" is posted with a `Cue::Extirpated` sound and recorded with its sector count and location. Other disjoint ranges of the lineage are unaffected.
- **Limits.** Persistence remembers kills only, so unloaded sectors are judged by generated spawns minus fallen. Bodies count by where they are: a creature bred or wandering in from outside the range does not block it, one bred inside does. Bases and what they breed are not spawns of the lineage. A truncated range (past the radius) is judged as if it ended there, and a flood that meets a cached range returns that range. Extirpation by hunger or predation counts, since the world has no memory of who is to blame.

## HUD, input and feel (built)

The design behind it is [FLOW.md](FLOW.md); the bindings are the README's table. The rule is that rendering owns no rules: every number the HUD draws comes from a pure, tested function in the library.

- **View-model** (`simulation/hud.rs`, `Game::hud()`): hull in ten segments (`segment_fill`), shield fraction, the three ability rings (`ability_ring`: locked, cooling with a fill, ready, active, or no-energy), the weapon icon (profile, level, fuel fraction of its material, dry, owned count), three cargo pips, five threat pips (`threat_pips`: nothing near gives none, else the verdict ladder from strong to outclassed, one more for a crowd hunting, calm civilizations never count), the standing meter, the region tag's fade (`region_alpha`: full for six seconds, then down to 30 percent), the chain, damage direction marks, the lure and the context hint line (`context_hints`, at most five keys).
- **Drawing** (`src/hud.rs`): gizmos laid out in logical pixels from the window's top left (`Screen`), at a UI scale that steps up with window height (`ui_scale_for`), so rings, pips, bars and the few small texts line up at every size. The ship's rings sit in world space and ignore camera zoom. Details, the key list, the bench and the settings are UI panels that scroll with the wheel when the window is small.
- **Chain** (`simulation/feel.rs`, `Streak`): kills, grazes and perfect parries within three seconds add 0.25 each to a score multiplier, up to x3. It changes score only.
- **Interact** (`simulation/interact.rs`): `interact_prompt` says what E would do and why not (a blocked pad verb waits behind a tithe that would work), `interact` does it. `PadHint::Build` is the new state: no kit, but the hold can pay for one.
- **Auto repair** (`pads.rs`, `tuning::AUTO_*`): after three seconds without damage, thrust, fire or beam, hull mends from metal; shield only below half and with more than 25 volatiles left. Silent, stops on any movement, off in the settings.
- **Shake** (`feel.rs`, `Trauma`): per-source trauma (`Shake`: hull 0.25, shield break 0.15, a big explosion within 600 units 0.3 scaled by distance, ram 0.15, apex 0.4, perfect parry 0.1, dash 0.05), each with its own cap, summed and clamped to one, decaying 1.8 a second, offset 10 pixels times its square, at most six shakes a second, no rotation. Firing, mining and pickups have no source. `shake_for_event` and `shake_for_cue` are the pure mapping; `src/juice.rs` feeds them and the camera applies the offset (HUD texts and the HUD itself stay put). Reduce effects turns it all off.
- **Hit stops** (`feel.rs`): the parry's 0.06 s, plus 0.03 s for killing a body with 150 or more hull (within 1400 units) and for a hull hit of 20 or more; one stop per half second in all, none while one runs.
- **Hit feel**: the ship's shot on a creature or station leaves a white tick (`EffectKind::Hit`) in place of the orange spark; a kill queues `FeelEvent::Kill` for a ring and a floating score; damage leaves a `HurtMark` (the angle comes from the nearest hostile shot or touching body taken before the step, `incoming_angle`) that shows as a red arc on the shield ring for 0.7 s; a low hull frames the window in red; a bench purchase queues `FeelEvent::Purchase` for a ring in the part's rarity color.
- **Next lure** (`simulation/lure.rs`): the first visit to each sector sends a free ping (`send_ping(true)`: no cooldown, at most one per six seconds, re-armed by a teleport or jump). Sounded echoes and an apex in reach are offered to one marker; `lure_score` is distance in sectors plus a handicap (lode 0, civilization 0.75, planetoid 1.5, apex 6), so the best wins and a far prize loses to a near one. The marker clears on arrival (320 units). Gold diamond on the target, or an edge arrow with kind and distance.

## Kinetic impacts (built)

`src/simulation/impact.rs` holds one pure function, `kinetic_damage(closing_speed, inverse_mass_a, inverse_mass_b)`: `0.00003 * 1/2 * reduced mass * (speed - 300)^2`, zero at or below 300 units per second (resting contact, schooling and separation jitter do nothing), speed clamped at 1400, damage capped at 160. It is symmetric, monotonic in speed and in either mass, deterministic, and a fixed body (wall, pinned stone, turret) counts as infinitely heavy. The contact solver applies it to every non-fling pair that closes fast enough: both bodies take it, the ship takes half (nothing under a lunatic field), a pair that has just struck is quiet for 0.35 s, and a planetoid hurts nothing. The ship's own ramming still meets the free-rock armour (`ROCK_HULL_FACTOR`), but a rock striking anything is not armoured, so a fast heavy rock cracks a fortress wall (enemy fire still never does) and a hard rock-on-rock hit breaks both. A Lunatic's throw is itself free, so it does not grind down its neighbours; the thrown body is the missile, and what it hits at 500 to 1000 units per second hurts in proportion to its mass. The ship's half share is trimmed by the PLATING skill. Rock shoving (mine, then ram; the grip and the dash whip) is built on this, see `simulation/shove.rs` and FLOW.md; Weaver webs tether free rocks (see Rare powers). Numbers are in `tuning` (`IMPACT_*`, `SHOVE_*`, `GRIP_*`, `WHIP_*`, `PLATING_*`).

## Charting, renewables and beacons (built)

- Which planetoids regrow is a pure hash of the spawn key (`mining::renewable`, about a third); the sonar's lode tier and the star map mark them, so a place worth returning to is readable from generation alone. Regrowth is game-time driven and persists through unloading (`Game::mined` plus a timestamp).
- The chart keeps what the ship has learned (a visit reveals everything but the predator count, a ping reveals what answered); beacons are the player's own persistent points and the only fast-travel targets. Neither changes generation.

## Legacy and wrecks (built)

- The legacy is the only thing that crosses runs besides the seed (and so the universe): a capped share of mined ore, one weapon at a low level, and wrecks. A wreck's looter is the civilization whose territory (`world::territory`) holds its sector, if it still stands; the delay is a hash of seed, sector and wreck id, counted in play time.


## Bounded curiosity discovery (built)

`simulation/discovery.rs` adds live Seamer pairs and moving or changing wells to base ping, and sealed organ relics to the existing LODE ECHO upgrade. [DISCOVERY.md](DISCOVERY.md) records the authoritative reconciliation, lifecycle, budgets, glyphs, and validation. Temporary echoes track actual positions and availability; the map remembers only discovered generated well anchors/modes and uncollected relic sites. Rifts and carried/released wells never become persistent destinations. Curiosity lures are fallback choices after existing starter lures. Relic provenance now records collection in the existing one-time memory. Generator version stays 19 because generation and offline map compatibility do not change.
